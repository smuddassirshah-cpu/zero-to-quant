- l19 (Jensen's inequality): hook: a share moves $+10\%$ or $-10\%$ with equal chance each day; the average daily return is 0, yet two days later the learner's £100 is worth £99 either way it went up and down; the average log return is $\tfrac12(\ln 1.1 + \ln 0.9) = \tfrac12\ln 0.99 \approx -0.00503$, below $\ln(1 + 0) = 0$; Jensen named, on a `plane` with $\ln(1 + x)$ and the chord between $x = -0.1$ and $x = 0.1$: for concave $g$, $E[g(X)] \le g(E[X])$, for convex $g$ the reverse, with equality only when $X$ is constant (or $g$ is linear where $X$ lives). Ladder: $E[X^2] \ge (E[X])^2$ is Jensen for $g(x) = x^2$, and the gap is the variance; $E[1/X] \ge 1/E[X]$ for a positive $X$ (pound-cost averaging: a fixed £100 a month buys more shares when the price is low; the average price paid is the harmonic mean, below the arithmetic mean of the prices; give a three-month example with exact numbers); a call payoff $(X - K)^+$ is convex, so $E[(X - K)^+] \ge (E[X] - K)^+$ (an option is worth at least its payoff at the expected price); Cauchy–Schwarz $|E[XY]| \le \sqrt{E[X^2]E[Y^2]}$, which gives $|\rho| \le 1$ (one screen, link l11). Trap: using Jensen in the wrong direction for a concave function; concluding $E[g(X)] = g(E[X])$ because $g$ is "nearly linear" (the drag is about $\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}$: 0.005 here); a Stat 110-style "which is larger, or can it go either way" item with four pairs (for example $E[e^X]$ against $e^{E[X]}$, $E[\sqrt{X}]$ against $\sqrt{E[X]}$, $E[X^3]$ against $E[X]^3$ for a positive $X$, and $E[X]E[Y]$ against $E[XY]$, which can go either way). Edge case: $g$ convex on one part and concave on another ($x^3$ for $X$ of either sign): no inequality holds in general. Derive: Jensen for a convex $g$ from the tangent line at $E[X]$ lying below the curve (limits l20), a justification per line. Transfer: volatility drag: a strategy with arithmetic mean return 1% a month and sd 6% compounds at about $1\% - \tfrac{0.06^2}{2} = 0.82\%$ a month (state it as the second-order approximation from taylor; compute the exact log-mean for a two-point return with that mean and sd and report it); one sentence on why Kelly maximises $E[\ln(\text{wealth})]$ rather than $E[\text{wealth}]$ (link limits l14).
