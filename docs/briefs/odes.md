# Level briefs: odes (Differential Equations, topic 1.11)

Course context. `odes` is the third course of topic 1.11 (after `complex` and `vector-calc`), 60 hours in 4 levels and 18 lessons, strand `quant` ("Desk quant and rates: pricing models, mean-reverting short rates."). It serves the differential-equations part of the topic 1.11 cover line in `docs/curriculum.md` (first-order equations, linear second-order equations, systems and phase planes, stability, the Laplace transform, numerical solution with Euler and Runge-Kutta, a mean-reverting equation as the drift of an interest rate model) and the exit line (the MIT 18.03 final at 70%; solve one equation numerically in NumPy and match its closed form to four decimal places, which is l18). MIT 18.03 (lectures and problem sets) is the core reference; every homework is pitched at an 18.03 final. The map is in `docs/briefs/breadth-plan.md` (the `### odes` section). Where quants use it, level by level: continuous compounding, discounting, annuities and the Vasicek and Ornstein-Uhlenbeck mean (level 1); a perpetual option's pricing equation, filters and overshoot (level 2); two-factor rate models, mean-reverting spreads and stability (level 3); present values as Laplace transforms and numerical schemes, the bridge to `numerics` (Stage 2: l11 step size, l12 stiff equations, l13 finite differences) and `pdes` (Stage 3: separation of variables builds on l2 and l7; l12 a four-decimal finite-difference pricer). Three facts every level agent needs. First, `arma` l12 and `coint` l7, cited as "builds on" for level 1, are Stage 2 (topic 2.2): the learner meets them after this course, so l5 must stand alone and only preview them; those lessons already state the half-life as $\ln 2/\kappa$ and call the spread an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, so match that notation. Second, `eigen` l14 (topic 1.4) already solves $\mathbf{u}' = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}$ by eigenvectors and the matrix exponential, with $\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix}-2&1\\1&-2\end{pmatrix}$ (eigenvalues $-1$ and $-3$) and "the slowest eigenvalue sets the half-life"; level 3 builds on it rather than repeating it. Third, `complex` is not written yet (only its map), so level 2 states Euler's formula where it needs it. `vector-calc` is a pure course, hidden on the "Quant path only" setting, so no lesson may rely on it. Engine limits known to the planner: `plane` has no slope-field or direction-field layer and no ODE integrator (a dragged start point cannot trace its own trajectory unless the trajectory has a closed form), and `walk` has no mean-reverting model; each level section says the workaround. Grader notes for every level: the expression grader reads a capital `N` as a built-in function, so never use `N` as a variable in an `expression` answer (write $n$ for a step count and $P$ for a population); `fmtNum` rounds numeric answers to 6 decimal places, so never ask for a `numeric` answer smaller than about $10^{-4}$ (ask for an error in units of $10^{-6}$, a ratio, or a count of steps instead).

- l14 (the Laplace transform): hook: the present value of £1 a year paid continuously for ever at rate 4% is $\int_0^\infty e^{-0.04t}\,dt = £25$; the learner slides the rate on a `plane` of $e^{-st}$ with its area shown. The Laplace transform named: $F(s) = \int_0^\infty e^{-st}f(t)\,dt$, so a present value at a continuously compounded rate $s$ is a Laplace transform. Ladder: $\mathcal{L}\{1\} = 1/s$ and $\mathcal{L}\{e^{at}\} = 1/(s - a)$ for $s > a$ from the definition; $\mathcal{L}\{t\} = 1/s^2$ by parts (integrals l10), so 0.25 at $s = 2$; $\mathcal{L}\{t^2\} = 2/s^3$; $\mathcal{L}\{\sin bt\} = b/(s^2 + b^2)$ (0.3 for $b = 3$, $s = 1$) and $\mathcal{L}\{\cos bt\} = s/(s^2 + b^2)$ (0.2 for $b = 2$, $s = 1$); linearity; the shift rule $\mathcal{L}\{e^{at}f\} = F(s - a)$; the derivative rule $\mathcal{L}\{f'\} = sF - f(0)$ by parts, and $\mathcal{L}\{f''\} = s^2F - sf(0) - f'(0)$ (the reason l15 works). Trap: a dividend of £5 a year growing at 2% and discounted at 6% is worth $\mathcal{L}\{5e^{0.02t}\}(0.06) = 5/0.04 = £125$ (the Gordon growth model), but the same formula at a discount rate of 1% gives a negative "price": the integral diverges for $s \le a$, so the formula does not apply; a second distractor multiplies transforms for a product of functions. Edge case: $e^{t^2}$ has no transform at any $s$. `code` screen: `laplace_numeric(f, s, T=50, n=200000)` by the trapezium rule on $[0, T]$ with NumPy (tests with `tol: { "abs": 1e-6 }`: $e^{-t}$ at $s = 1$ gives 0.5, $t$ at $s = 2$ gives 0.25, $\sin 3t$ at $s = 1$ gives 0.3; the planner's trapezium values were about 0.50000001, 0.24999999 and 0.29999998), and `pv_growing(c, g, r)` returning $c/(r - g)$ and raising `ValueError` when $r \le g$.
