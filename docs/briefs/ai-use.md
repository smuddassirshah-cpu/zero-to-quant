# Level briefs: ai-use (Using AI well, topic 0.8)

Course header. Topic 0.8 "AI from nothing" (Stage 0, track `ml`, strand `ai`), the second course after `ai-basics`. Binding plan: `docs/briefs/ai-strand-curriculum.md` section 0.8 and `docs/reviews/review-ai-strand.md` F4 (each idea has one home: prompting lives only here, so `llm-apps` never teaches it; alignment and safety research live in `ai-safety`, Stage 3, so this course names risks to an everyday user and previews alignment in plain words only). The course has three levels (the validator needs 3 to 5): how a chatbot is made and why it makes things up; asking and checking; the risks. The course never gates the quant path (`strand: "ai"`). References and what each is for:
- Andrej Karpathy, Intro to Large Language Models (2023) and Deep Dive into LLMs like ChatGPT (2025): the story of pretraining, fine-tuning and human feedback (level 1). Use them for the shape of the story only; quote no figures from them (sizes, costs, token counts) unless you can check the figure in the source, and say "about" with a date when you do.
- Anthropic, prompt engineering documentation: the habits in level 2 l5 (say the task, give context, show the format, give an example, let the model say it does not know). Describe the habits in your own words; do not copy text.
- Elements of AI (University of Helsinki, free): its chapter on the societal implications, for the voice of l8 (bias from data, privacy).

Who the learner is. Stage 0 only (arithmetic, percentages, Stage 0 algebra, first probabilities, a first Python script) plus `ai-basics` levels 1 to 4: a model is a rule found from examples and judged on unseen ones (L1), it learns by lowering an error (L2), neurons and layers (L3), and next-word tables, sampling and temperature (L4). No maths beyond that is needed or allowed here. This course has few numbers: its screens are mostly `choice`, `multi`, `order`, `tap-regions` and `table` tasks on realistic chatbot transcripts that you write. Every transcript is invented by you and labelled as an example; never present one as a real product's output, never name a real person, and never imitate a real company's interface. Where a lesson needs a product, say "a chatbot".

Runtime. Python in Pyodide 0.27.7, standard library only, and code only where it earns its place (l6 may have one `code` screen that recomputes a chatbot's sum). A graded case costs well under 1 s; no `budget` is needed. Numbers used (computed twice in `docs/briefs/ai-s0s1-scripts/stage0.py`, exact fractions and floats): from the `ai-basics` level 4 text (`the cat sat on the mat . the cat ate the jam . the dog sat on the cat .`), the sentence "the dog sat on the mat ." never appears, yet the bigram table writes it from "the" with chance $\tfrac16 \times 1 \times 1 \times 1 \times \tfrac16 \times 1 = \tfrac{1}{36}$ (about 0.028), because every pair in it is in the text. £2,400 at 3% a year compounded for 5 years is £2,782.26 ($2400 \times 1.03^5$); simple interest gives £2,760 (the slip a chatbot might make: $2400 \times (1 + 5 \times 0.03)$); the estimate "15% and a little more" gives between £2,760 and £2,784. 17% of £4,850 is £824.50 (10% is 485, 5% is 242.50, 2% is 97).
