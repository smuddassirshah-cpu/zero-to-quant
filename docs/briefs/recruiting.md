# Level briefs: recruiting (Getting the Internship, topic 0.7)

Course context. `recruiting` is the only course of topic 0.7, Getting hired (track `speed`, 15 hours, no topic exam). The planner's design is `docs/briefs/interview-plan.md` section (b) `recruiting`, section (e) and "User decisions (29 October 2026)" at its end; the firm facts come from `docs/briefs/interview-gaps.md` section 1 and from the brief-writer's own searches listed in the fact table below. The map is `app/content/courses/recruiting/course.json` (3 levels, 15 lessons, art `check`, skills `rk-landscape`, `rk-apply`, `rk-assess`, `rk-behave`, `rk-offer`). The topic exit line in `app/content/curriculum.json` is binding: "A one-page CV, three "why us" answers, six behavioural stories and a dated target list, each checked against its rubric." The homework of the three levels produces exactly those four artefacts. The reference text is QuantVault's quant internship timeline and Quantt's UK guide to quant internships (named in `curriculum.json`), plus each firm's own early-careers pages.

Who the learner is. L1 is sat in Stage 0, in the first weeks, so the learner may know only school maths, Zetamac-style mental arithmetic (`mental`) and a little Python (`first-python`). L2 and L3 are sat about four months before applying, so a learner on the full path then has most of Stages 1 and 2 and some of Stage 3. Write L1 for the beginner; write L2 and L3 so that a learner with fewer projects still finishes every artefact. The learner works two cycles at once (user decision, 29 October 2026): **summer 2027**, whose applications are open now and closing, applied for opportunistically; and **summer 2028**, the full cycle, prepared properly. Lead with the UK and European cycle (spring weeks, insight days, off-cycle internships, London, Amsterdam and Dublin offices, £ figures); give the US cycle as a comparison, never as the default.

Rules shared by every level of this course (each level section repeats the essentials):

- **Firm facts.** Name firms in plain text only. Never use a logo, a firm's colours, a firm's typeface, a mock-up of a firm's page, or wording that reads as the firm speaking. Every firm fact carries "as reported" (or "official" when it comes from the firm's own page) and a month and year, and a source line in the screen's caption or explanation, for example "Source: Optiver EU campus recruiting Q&A, optiver.com, read October 2026." Prose does not render links, so write the source as plain text (site and page name), with a URL in backticks only where the page name is not enough. All firm-by-firm process facts live in **l4** (and later in the mock exam descriptions, which are not this course's job). Other lessons may name a firm only to illustrate a pattern already sourced in l4 (for example "a bank's recorded video round, l4"), with the same "as reported" date. Paraphrase every reported question; never quote one.
- **Freshness.** Every firm fact here is dated October 2026. Formats change every cycle, and deadlines in this brief will pass. Teach the pattern (when windows open, how rolling review works, what each firm type tests) and use dated facts as examples of it. Each firm-fact screen tells the learner to check the firm's own page before acting. Raise a backlog bullet asking for E19 (`asOf` and `sources` on lessons) if you need it; until then the date is in prose.
- **No audio recording.** Spoken practice means answering aloud, alone or to a friend, then self-marking against a rubric. Never ask the learner to record themselves in the app. A phone timer is fine to suggest; the app has no countdown in lessons (E6 is not built).
- **Where `longform` can go.** The validator allows `longform` only in homework and exams (`app/tools/validate.mjs`: "longform belongs in homework and exams"); a lesson screen, a lesson quiz or a review item with `longform` is an error. So lessons and reviews train judgement on answers the course supplies (pick the stronger answer, tap the weak line, order the parts, tick which rubric lines a model answer meets with `multi`), and the learner's own artefacts are written or spoken in homework, self-marked against a 10-point rubric. Say this in each lesson's last screen: "You write your own in homework n."
- **Homework produces real artefacts.** The learner keeps what they write. Each artefact item's prompt says "Write this as you will send it" and the model answer is an example of a different, invented candidate (never the learner's own details, never a real person). Homework 1: the dated target list and a two-cycle calendar. Homework 2: the one-page CV, three "why this firm" paragraphs, a two-minute project account. Homework 3: six behavioural stories. Each is one `longform` item with a 10-point rubric and a full model answer. Every homework also needs one `order` item (the course has no `track`, so the validator asks for a code, order or expression item) and one item at difficulty 5.
- **Widgets.** Mostly `choice` (with `shuffle: true` unless the options are ordered), `multi` ("Select all that apply" in the question line), `order` (with `distractors` for a step that does not belong, each with a `why`), `table` (editable cells; a text answer is graded as text, so give `accept` spellings), `tap-regions` on a `table` canvas with `tap: "rows"` (the weak CV line, the sentence with no number) or on a `console` canvas (a transcript, one line per turn), `numeric` (days to a deadline, pay over ten weeks, expected values), `drag-point` on a `number-line` (placing a step on the calendar), and `place` in `boxes` mode on a `boxes` canvas (sorting short cards into containers). Visuals must earn their place: the calendar on `number-line`, the firm table on `table`, transcripts on `console`, task cards on `boxes`. No `plane` unless a lesson plots something real.
- **Engine limits to avoid.** (1) A `derive` screen takes only `expression`, `order` or `choice`. (2) A `table` with 5 or more columns scrolls sideways at 390 px; keep every table to 4 columns or fewer (put detail in the explanation or a second screen). (3) In `place`, one slot holds one chip; to sort several cards into one category use `place` in `boxes` mode (containers hold many items), not slots. Box cards are as wide as their label, so keep card text to three or four words. (4) Set `shuffle: true` on every `choice` and `multi` whose options are not ordered. (5) `playthrough --mobile` screenshots only the viewport, so scroll and screenshot long tables and transcripts yourself before judging the layout. (6) The `table` canvas has no tap-to-open rows: the "tap a row, see the detail" idea in `interview-plan.md` becomes a tap on a row graded by `tap-regions`, with the detail in the explanation patch or on the next screen. (7) No links in prose (above). (8) No timers in lessons (above).
- **Voice.** British English (programme, behaviour, organise, CV), no exclamation marks, no emoji, no em-dashes, sentences under 20 words, explanations of at most three sentences, second person for instructions and "we" when reasoning, £ for money (give euros or dollars only when a source states them, then convert nothing). A firm's own programme name keeps its spelling ("First-Year Trading and Technology Program"); the validator's American-spelling list does not include "program", but use "programme" in your own prose. Every lesson still has a puzzle-first hook, a trap screen, an edge case and a transfer screen (`docs/LEVEL-BRIEF.md`); in this course the transfer is the learner's own application (a real posting, their own calendar, their own CV line) or a quant idea applied to recruiting (expected value of a guess, the chance of no offer across independent applications).
- **Canvas early.** The validator warns about a `problem` with no canvas before the ladder ends. Give every early problem a canvas (a `table` of facts, a `console` transcript, `boxes` cards, a `number-line`), even when the task is a `choice`.
- **Numbers.** The brief-writer computed every number below twice (by hand and in Python, 29 October 2026). Recompute each in your scratchpad before using it. Date arithmetic is counted from 29 October 2026; if you author on a later date, keep 29 October 2026 as the lesson's stated "as of" date so the numbers stay true.
