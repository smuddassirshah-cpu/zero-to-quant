# Level briefs: pairs-rep (Replicating Pairs Trading, topic 3.1)

Course context. `pairs-rep` is the second Stage 3 paper replication (topic 3.1, Research papers, track `ml`; `docs/curriculum.md` section 3.1: replicate one classic, reproduce its main table, extend it to data from after publication; exit: one replication on GitHub with an out-of-sample extension and a plain note on how much of the effect survived). The paper is Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst (2006), "Pairs Trading: Performance of a Relative-Value Arbitrage Rule", Review of Financial Studies 19(3): daily CRSP data, 1962 to 2002; at the start of each month, every share that traded every day of the past 12 months has its total-return index normalised to 1 at the start of that 12-month formation period; each share is matched with the partner that minimises the sum of squared differences between the two normalised price paths (the distance method); the 5 and the 20 closest pairs (and further bands such as pairs 101 to 120) are traded over the next 6 months: a pair opens, long the cheaper and short the dearer with $1 on each side, when the normalised prices diverge by more than two standard deviations of their formation-period spread, and closes at the next crossing of the prices or at the end of the trading period; a pair may reopen after it closes; returns are reported on committed capital (every nominated pair counts, traded or not) and on employed capital (only pairs that opened); a "wait one day" version opens and closes a day after the signal, to remove the bid-ask bounce; six overlapping portfolios run at any time, as in Jegadeesh and Titman. What every level may quote as "the paper reports" (the agents have no network and cannot open the PDF, so quote only these): annualised excess returns of up to 11% for self-financing portfolios of pairs (the abstract); the profits survive conservative estimates of transaction costs; waiting a day cuts returns materially but leaves them positive; profits were lower in the later years of the sample; pairs returns are not explained by standard risk factors and share a common latent factor. One later paper is named once where it is used: Do and Faff (2010), "Does simple pairs trading still work?", which found the decline continued and was driven by fewer pairs converging. The generator (verbatim in a `prelude` wherever a screen needs data; describe it in words to the learner until level 1 l5, where they read it):

```python
import numpy as np
def pairs_panel(seed=2006, n_ind=10, per_ind=10, n_pairs=20, days=3024, half_life=8.0, ou_sd=0.008,
                rw_sd=0.015, break_day=1512, break_frac=0.8, half_spread=0.005):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    N = n_ind * per_ind
    ind = np.repeat(np.arange(n_ind), per_ind)
    mkt = np.cumsum(0.0003 + 0.010 * rng.standard_normal(days))
    indf = np.cumsum(0.008 * rng.standard_normal((days, n_ind)), axis=0)
    phi = 0.5 ** (1.0 / half_life)
    u = np.cumsum(rw_sd * rng.standard_normal((days, N)), axis=0)        # unpaired: random walks
    common = np.cumsum(rw_sd * rng.standard_normal((days, n_pairs)), axis=0)
    eps = ou_sd * rng.standard_normal((days, 2 * n_pairs))
    a = np.zeros((days, 2 * n_pairs))
    for t in range(1, days):
        a[t] = phi * a[t - 1] + eps[t]                                   # mean-reverting gaps
    broken = rng.random(n_pairs) < break_frac
    drift = np.cumsum(rw_sd * rng.standard_normal((days, 2 * n_pairs)), axis=0)
    for k in range(n_pairs):
        for j in (2 * k, 2 * k + 1):
            x = common[:, k] + a[:, j]
            if broken[k]:                                                # the pair stops co-moving
                x = x + np.where(np.arange(days) >= break_day, drift[:, j] - drift[break_day, j], 0.0)
            u[:, j] = x
    pair_ind = np.repeat(np.arange(n_ind), 2)[:n_pairs]
    ind[:2 * n_pairs] = np.repeat(pair_ind, 2)                           # two planted pairs an industry
    logp = np.log(50.0) + mkt[:, None] + indf[:, ind] + u
    mid = np.exp(logp)
    bounce = np.where(rng.random((days, N)) < 0.5, 1 + half_spread, 1 - half_spread)
    close = mid * bounce                                                 # closes at bid or ask
    return close, mid, ind, broken
```
