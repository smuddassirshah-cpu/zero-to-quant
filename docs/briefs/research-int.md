## Shape, voice and shared rules (every level)

- **Lesson shape.** The `puzzles` and `brainteasers` shape: a quick hook the learner acts on before any rule is named, a worked second and third question of rising difficulty, one `trap` screen built on the answer candidates blurt, one edge case, a `derive` screen where a derivation exists (the lesson text names it, as in l1, l2 and l5), a `transfer` screen in a real quant setting (a desk, a signal, a dataset, a P&L), and a closing screen titled "The follow-up": the same question extended the way an interviewer extends it (a `ladder` screen, since lessons have no `parts`). Each lesson states a pace in prose ("about two minutes; say your reasoning aloud first") and carries `pace` on its drill screens. 8 to 12 screens, 3 to 5 quiz items, `summary`, as `docs/LEVEL-BRIEF.md` says. The validator requires `minutes` 10 to 15 per lesson, so set `minutes: 15` everywhere and put the real time in the lesson's first prose ("about 40 minutes with the code screens" for l14, "twenty-five minutes" for l19); count the `pace` figures in the report.
- **Voice.** British English, no exclamation marks, no em-dashes, sentences under 20 words, explanations of at most three sentences, £ for money (a bare £ inside `$...$` is `\\text{£}`), second person. The interview-answer sentences the lessons model are short and spoken: "The two slopes multiply to r squared."
- **Grading reasoning.** Free-form arguments are never graded. Use `numeric` (fractions accepted; write `accept` as "Any form equal to 0.4, or 0.4 ± 0.0005"), `choice` and `multi` with `shuffle: true`, `table` with `edit` cells, `order` with `distractors` (each with a `why`) and `same`, `tap-regions`, `expression` only for closed forms. Whole answers go to `longform` homework items with a 10-point rubric and a full model answer (the validator refuses `longform` in a review). Each distractor is a real blurt and its `why` names what the learner did ("You multiplied the two slopes by mistake...").
- **Tags and pace.** Every drill screen, quiz item, review item and homework item carries `tags` (the first is the family, kebab-case; families are listed per lesson) and, where the brief says so, `pace` in seconds. Items also carry `skills`. Families are the keys the bank and the mock papers draw by (E3 below).
- **Timed reviews.** `review-1` and `review-2` use `timed: { "seconds": N, "lockAtZero": true }` (existing engine item 454): 540 and 600 seconds. An item with E11 `parts` (R1.2 is the only one in a timed review) counts unopened parts as missed when the clock locks (engine requirement C-parts-credit). `review-3` and `review-4` are untimed and show `pace` only, since cases and data tasks are read slowly.
- **Two kinds of number.** Numbers in this brief come from two sources. Closed forms are stated with the working. Seeded results (marked "seeded") come from a script and a fixed seed; the level agent recomputes them with the script named per level and pastes the arrays into canvas params, so a canvas never depends on a random stream.
- **No overlap.** Cite existing lessons by id and go past them with new numbers. `estimation` l4-bias-against-variance, `stat-learning` l3-bias-variance and `regression` l2-slope-formula teach the ideas; this course teaches the interview form: speed, the trap, the follow-up, saying it aloud.
- **Free resources.** Name them once, in l20: practice sets and bank decks inside the app, and the learner's own take-home.

- **l1-two-regressions** (tags `two-regressions`, `duplicated-data`, `measurement-error`; pace 120 s a question). Builds on `regression` l2-slope-formula, l3-how-much-explained, l5-how-sure. Method: the slope of y on x is r × sy/sx, the slope of x on y is r × sx/sy, so the two slopes multiply to r squared, and both fits have the same R squared. Hook (`scatter`, five points (1, 2), (2, 6), (3, 4), (4, 10), (5, 8), `fit: true`; `numeric`): "The least squares line of y on x has what slope?" 1.6 (means 3 and 6, Sxx 10, Syy 40, Sxy 16; intercept 1.2). Worked: (1) the hook; (2) regress x on y: slope 0.4, intercept 0.6; drawn in the same (x, y) plane it is the line y = 2.5x − 1.5, so a second line (`state.lines`, slope 2.5) is steeper than the first, `numeric` 2.5; (3) r = 16/√(10 × 40) = 0.8, r² = 0.64 = 1.6 × 0.4, and sy/sx = 2 sits between 1.6 and 2.5 (√(1.6 × 2.5) = 2), `numeric` 0.64. Trap: "If the slope of y on x is 1.6, the slope of x on y is 1/1.6 = 0.625" (`numeric`, `wrong` 0.625, `why`: the two lines agree only when the points lie exactly on a line). Edge: r = 0 gives both slopes 0, two perpendicular lines; r = −0.8 gives −1.6 and −0.4. Generalise: b_yx × b_xy = r² ≤ 1, so slopes 0.8 and 1.5 together are impossible (product 1.2) while 0.5 and 1.2 are possible (r = √0.6 = 0.7746). Derive (`order`, distractor "take the reciprocal"): Sxy/Sxx times Sxy/Syy is Sxy² / (Sxx Syy) = r². Transfer: a stock with volatility 30% and an index with 15%, correlation 0.5: the stock's beta on the index is 0.5 × 30/15 = 1.0 and the index on the stock is 0.5 × 15/30 = 0.25 (a beta is not symmetric). Follow-up (ladder, two parts as two screens): (a) "Now every row is written twice." The data x = 1 to 10, y = 2.8, 4.2, 3.9, 3.4, 5.1, 4.8, 7.7, 11.1, 8.2, 8.8 (seeded): slope 0.7915 and R² 0.7548 do not change; the standard error of the slope falls by √((n−2)/(2n−2)) = √(8/18) = 2/3, so the t statistic rises by 1.5, from 4.962 to 7.443, and the p-value from about 0.001 to under 0.000001 (0.0011 to 6.7e-7), although nothing new was learned. (b) "x is measured with noise." With var(x) = 4 and noise variance 1 the fitted slope is 2 × 4/(4 + 1) = 1.6 when the true slope is 2 (attenuation factor 0.8).
- **l2-correlation-limits** (tags `correlation-bounds`, `psd-check`; pace 150 s). Builds on `eigen` l7-covariance-psd. Method: a set of correlations is possible only if the matrix has no negative eigenvalue; for three variables with corr(X, Y) = a and corr(Y, Z) = b, corr(X, Z) = c lies in ab ± √((1 − a²)(1 − b²)), which is the condition det = 1 + 2abc − a² − b² − c² ≥ 0. Hook (`plane` with x = b, y = c, a fixed at 0.8, a `heat` layer of 1 − a² − x² − y² + 2axy, a `drag-point`): "With a = b = 0.8, drag c to the smallest value that is still possible." 0.28 (tol 0.01). The heat is flat along the boundary curve, so grade the point itself (a `target` on b = 0.8, c = 0.28, or an `expr` target on c), and check in playthrough that c = 0.285 passes and c = 0.2 fails; otherwise key on a `line` target. Worked: (1) a = b = 0.8: [0.28, 1] (0.64 ± 0.36); (2) a = b = 0.9: [0.62, 1] (0.81 ± 0.19); (3) a = 0.9, b = −0.9: [−1, −0.62]; a = 0.6, b = 0.8: [0, 0.96] (0.48 ± 0.48); a = b = 0.5: [−0.5, 1]; a = b = 0: [−1, 1]. Trap: "Both are 0.9, so X and Z must correlate at least 0.9 × 0.9 = 0.81" (`numeric`, `wrong` 0.81: the true floor is 0.62). Edge: equal correlation ρ across n variables needs ρ ≥ −1/(n − 1): −1/2 for three, −1/3 for four, the eigenvalue 1 + (n − 1)ρ reaching zero. Generalise: the formula and the determinant. Derive (`order`): write the 3 × 3 matrix, require det ≥ 0, treat it as a quadratic in c, take the roots. Transfer: three desks, equities-rates 0.6 and rates-credit 0.8, so equities-credit between 0 and 0.96; a vendor matrix with 0.9, 0.9 and −0.5 has determinant −1.68 and smallest eigenvalue −0.5471, so it is not a correlation matrix. Follow-up: "And for four assets?" the equicorrelation floor −1/3.
- **l3-estimators-on-the-spot** (tags `estimator-bias`, `max-of-uniform`, `variance-divisor`, `shrinkage-estimator`; pace 180 s). Builds on `estimation` l3-right-target, l4-bias-against-variance, l6-maximising. Method: write the estimator, take its mean for the bias and its spread for the variance, then compare mean squared errors (bias² + variance). Hook (`bars` `sim`, outcome `maxn(9, u(0, 10))`, trials 10, 100, 1000; `numeric`): "Nine draws are uniform on 0 to a hidden θ. Does the largest draw overshoot or undershoot θ = 10 on average?"
