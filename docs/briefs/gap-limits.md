- l17 (the mean value theorem): hook: a stock goes from £100 at 10:00 to £106 at 13:00 with no gaps; the learner drags a point along the seeded path to find a moment when the instantaneous speed was exactly the average, £2 an hour (a `plane` with the secant drawn and a tangent riding the point); Rolle's theorem named after a ladder on $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ on $[-\sqrt3, \sqrt3]$ (ends both 0, flat tangents at $x = \pm 1$); the MVT as Rolle tilted: on $[0, 2]$ the secant slope of $x^3 - 3x$ is $\tfrac{2 - 0}{2} = 1$, and $3c^2 - 3 = 1$ gives $c = \tfrac{2}{\sqrt3} \approx 1.1547$ (the learner computes it); the two hypotheses (continuous on $[a, b]$, differentiable inside) and what goes wrong without each: $|x|$ on $[-1, 1]$ has a secant slope of 0 and no flat tangent; consequences as a ladder: $f' = 0$ everywhere means $f$ is constant, $f' > 0$ means increasing, and the bound $|f(b) - f(a)| \le M|b - a|$ when $|f'| \le M$ (for example $|\sin a - \sin b| \le |a - b|$). Trap: reading the MVT as "the slope at the midpoint equals the average" (on $x^3 - 3x$ over $[0, 2]$ the midpoint slope is 0, not 1); assuming $c$ is unique. Edge case: a function with a jump (a price gap at the open), where no such $c$ need exist. Derive: Rolle from the extreme value theorem and the flat tangent at an interior extreme (l13), then the MVT by subtracting the secant line, a justification per line. Transfer: an option's delta stays between 0.4 and 0.6 while the stock moves between £95 and £105; the MVT bounds the option's change over a £10 move between £4 and £6, which is how a desk bounds a P&L from a Greek; one sentence that taylor l11's remainder and the FTC proof in integrals both rest on this theorem.
- l18 (related rates): hook: a 5 m ladder slides; its foot moves out at 0.5 m/s; how fast does the top fall when the foot is 3 m out? The learner first guesses (most say 0.5), then reads the answer off a `plane` animation with a slider on time; the method named: write the equation that links the quantities ($x^2 + y^2 = 25$), differentiate both sides in $t$ (each variable picks up its own rate, as in l12 each $y$ picked up $y'$), substitute the instant last: $2x x' + 2y y' = 0$ gives $y' = -\tfrac{x x'}{y} = -\tfrac{3 \times 0.5}{4} = -0.375$ m/s. Ladder: a position's value $V = qS$ with $q = 1{,}000$ shares being sold at 50 a minute and $S = \text{£}20$ rising £0.01 a minute: $V' = q'S + qS' = -1{,}000 + 10 = -\text{£}990$ a minute (product rule in time); variance and volatility: $v = \sigma^2$, so $\sigma' = \tfrac{v'}{2\sigma}$: implied variance rising 0.004 a day at $\sigma = 0.2$ means volatility rises 0.01 (one vol point) a day; the l15 bond at $y = 4\%$: $P(y) = 100(1 + y)^{-5} = \text{£}82.19$, $P'(0.04) = -395.16$, so a yield rising 2 bp a day moves the price about $-\text{£}0.079$ a day. Trap: substituting the instant's values before differentiating (the constants then have zero rate and the answer is 0); dropping the sign of a falling quantity. Edge case: the ladder's top as $y \to 0$, where the formula says the speed blows up (the model breaks, not physics; one sentence). Generalise: the four steps (draw and name, link, differentiate in $t$, substitute) as an `order` item. Transfer: a desk holds £10m of a bond whose yield rises 3 bp a day and duration is 7: the loss rate, with the link to l15's sensitivity.
- l19 (derivatives of inverse functions): hook: l11's reflection picture again, now for a general increasing $f$: a tangent of slope $a$ at $(x, f(x))$ reflects to slope $\tfrac{1}{a}$ at $(f(x), x)$; the learner drags a point on $f(x) = x^3 + x$ and reads $(f^{-1})'(2) = \tfrac{1}{f'(1)} = \tfrac14$ (since $f(1) = 2$); the rule named: $(f^{-1})'(y) = \tfrac{1}{f'(f^{-1}(y))}$, derived by implicit differentiation of $f(g(y)) = y$ (link l12). Ladder: $\arctan$: $y = \arctan x$ means $\tan y = x$, so $y' \sec^2 y = 1$ and $y' = \tfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$ (use $\sec^2 = 1 + \tan^2$); $\arcsin$: $y' = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$ on $(-1, 1)$, with the sign of $\cos y$ justified by the range $[-\tfrac{\pi}{2}, \tfrac{\pi}{2}]$; $\arccos$ in one line as $-\arcsin'$; a chain-rule item, $\tfrac{d}{dx}\arctan(2x) = \tfrac{2}{1 + 4x^2}$. Trap: writing $(f^{-1})'(y) = \tfrac{1}{f'(y)}$, evaluating at the wrong point (on $x^3 + x$ at $y = 2$ this gives $\tfrac{1}{13}$, not $\tfrac14$); confusing $\sin^{-1} x$ with $\tfrac{1}{\sin x}$. Edge case: an inverse with a vertical tangent where $f' = 0$ ($x^3$ at 0: its inverse $\sqrt[3]{x}$ has infinite slope). Transfer: implied volatility is the inverse of the pricing function $\sigma \mapsto C(\sigma)$: with vega £0.40 per vol point, a 10p rise in the option price means about 0.25 vol points, $\tfrac{d\sigma}{dC} = \tfrac{1}{\text{vega}}$; and a second transfer on quantiles: the 99% quantile of the standard normal is $q = 2.3263$ with density $\phi(q) = 0.02665$, so $q'(p) = \tfrac{1}{\phi(q)} \approx 37.5$: a VaR level is far more sensitive to the confidence level in the tail (state $\phi(q)$ as given, since normal densities come in topic 1.5). End with one sentence: $\tfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$ and $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$ come back as integrals in Integrals level 4.
- l20 (concavity and curve sketching): hook: two price paths with the same start, end and slope at the start, one bending up and one down; the learner taps which one a convex payoff profits from; concave up and down named by the sign of $f''$ (the slope's slope, l13), an inflection point as a change of sign of $f''$; explore: a `pair` with $f(x) = x e^{-x}$ above and $f''$ below with a shared $x$ slider: $f' = (1 - x)e^{-x}$ (maximum at $x = 1$, height $e^{-1} \approx 0.3679$), $f'' = (x - 2)e^{-x}$ (inflection at $x = 2$, height $2e^{-2} \approx 0.2707$). Ladder: a sign chart on a `number-line` for $f'$ and $f''$ of $x^3 - 3x$ (inflection at 0); a full sketch of $g(x) = \tfrac{x^2}{x^2 - 1}$: vertical asymptotes at $\pm 1$, horizontal asymptote $y = 1$ (l4), $g'(x) = \tfrac{-2x}{(x^2 - 1)^2}$ (local maximum at $(0, 0)$), $g''(x) = \tfrac{2(3x^2 + 1)}{(x^2 - 1)^3}$ (concave down on $(-1, 1)$, up outside); the learner places the features and then sketches with the `sketch` widget. Trap: calling $x = 0$ an inflection point of $x^4$ because $f''(0) = 0$ (no sign change); calling $\pm 1$ on $g$ inflection points (the concavity changes, but $g$ is not defined there). Edge case: $f''$ changes sign where $f''$ does not exist ($x^{1/3}$ at 0, a vertical tangent). Generalise: the sketching checklist (domain, intercepts, asymptotes, $f'$ signs, $f''$ signs, plot the points) as an `order` item. Transfer: a long call's value is convex in the stock price (gamma, the second derivative, is positive), so a move either way gains against the tangent hedge; a log-utility investor's $\ln w$ is concave, so a fair gamble lowers expected utility; the normal density $e^{-x^2/2}$ has inflection points at $x = \pm 1$, one standard deviation (compute $f''$); one sentence previewing Jensen's inequality in Random Variables level 5.
