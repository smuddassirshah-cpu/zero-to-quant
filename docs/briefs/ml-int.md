- **Figures** (`mlint.py`, `dd2.py`; second methods named):
  - Backprop by hand: x = (1, 2), W₁ = [[1, −1], [0.5, 1]], ReLU, w₂ = (2, −1), target 1, loss ½(ŷ − y)². Then h = (−1, 2.5), a = (0, 2.5), ŷ = −2.5, loss 6.125; ∂L/∂ŷ = −3.5, ∂L/∂w₂ = (0, −8.75), ∂L/∂a = (−7, 3.5), the ReLU passes only the second unit, ∂L/∂W₁ = [[0, 0], [3.5, 7]] (second method: central differences give the same to 6 places).
  - Adam's first step: with bias correction the step is lr × g/|g|, so 0.001 for g = 10⁻⁴, 1 and 100 at lr = 0.001 (second method: the update coded with the moving averages); without it the first step is 0.1/√0.001 = 3.1623 times larger (closed form and the coded update agree).
  - Double descent (min-norm least squares on random ReLU features: 40 training points in 10 dimensions, target tanh(x · w) with ‖w‖ about 1, noise sd 0.1, 2,000 test points; median test error over 20 seeds): 0.249 at 10 features, 0.250 at 20, 0.355 at 30, 0.770 at 36, a peak of 7.63 at 40 (as many features as points), 0.589 at 44, 0.248 at 60, 0.120 at 100, 0.0644 at 400 and 0.0646 at 2,000. Second method: `np.linalg.lstsq`, which also returns the minimum-norm solution, gives the same medians. With noise sd 0.2 the peak is 8.43 and the error at 2,000 features 0.122.
  - Quickfire: He initialisation for fan-in 512 has sd √(2/512) = 0.0625; the loss at initialisation of a 65-way softmax is ln 65 = 4.1744 and of a 27-way one ln 27 = 3.2958; inverted dropout with p = 0.2 scales kept units by 1/0.8 = 1.25; the dot product of two independent 64-dimensional standard normal vectors has sd 8 (simulation of 20,000 pairs: 8.037), and 1.005 after dividing by √64.
- **Canvases.** `graph` (the two-layer network, values then gradients), `series` (test error against features on a log axis, the peak marked), `table`, `bars` (step sizes with and without correction).
- **l1-backprop-on-a-whiteboard** (tags `backprop-by-hand`; pace 300 s). Builds on `backprop` L3 (recap one sentence). Hook: the network above on `graph`; the learner fills the forward values in a `table` before any rule is named. Ladder: the forward pass; ∂L/∂ŷ and ∂L/∂w₂; ∂L/∂W₁ through the gate. Trap: passing gradient through a ReLU whose input was negative (`numeric` with the slip [[−7, −14], [3.5, 7]] as a `wrong` value). Edge: a ReLU input of exactly 0 (the subgradient convention; say which one you use). Generalise: the same steps in matrix form, δ = (W₂ᵀ δ₂) ⊙ 1[h > 0], derived on a `derive` screen. Transfer: an interviewer at a fund changes the loss to log loss; the only change is ∂L/∂ŷ. Follow-up: "What changes with tanh?" (`choice`: the gate becomes 1 − a²). Code screen: `two_layer_grads(x, W1, w2, y)` returning (loss, dW1, dw2) (tests: the figures above exactly; central differences on a second supplied case to 1e-6).
- **l2-why-adam** (tags `optimisers`; pace 180 s). Builds on `nn-train` L1 (recap one sentence). Hook: three parameters with gradients 10⁻⁴, 1 and 100; after one Adam step, which moved furthest? (All moved 0.001.) Explore: the two moving averages, the bias correction, the per-parameter scale; AdamW's decoupled weight decay against an L2 term inside Adam's gradient. Ladder: the first step with correction; without it (3.1623 times larger); the effective step when gradients are noisy (m̂ small against √v̂). Trap: "Adam's step is proportional to the gradient" (`choice`). Edge: ε matters when v̂ is near zero (a parameter that is almost never updated). Generalise: when SGD with momentum is preferred (reported to generalise better on some vision tasks; well-tuned schedules matter more than the optimiser; say "reported"). Transfer: a fund's model retrained daily; the optimiser state carried over or reset. Follow-up: "Why does weight decay differ in Adam?" (`order`: the steps of AdamW's update). Code screen: `adam_first_step(g, lr, beta1, beta2, eps, corrected)` (tests: the four figures above; a zero gradient gives a zero step).
- **l3-double-descent** (tags `double-descent`, `bias-variance`; pace 240 s). Builds on `research-int` l6-bias-variance-aloud (the U-shaped picture; recap only). Hook: on `series`, test error against the number of features with the right half hidden; the learner predicts what happens past 40. Explore: at p = n the least-squares fit must pass through every noisy point, and the minimum-norm solution has a huge norm; past it, many solutions fit, and the minimum-norm one gets smoother as p grows. Ladder: read the peak (7.63 at 40); the second descent (0.0644 at 400, below the first minimum 0.249 at 10); what noise does (sd 0.2: the peak 8.43, the floor 0.122). Trap: "more parameters always overfit" (`choice`). Edge: with ridge regularisation tuned at each p the peak almost disappears (the level's script checks this twice before the lesson states it). Generalise: Belkin et al. and Nakkiran et al. (epoch-wise and model-wise double descent), read only. Transfer: why huge networks generalise at all, and why that does not license a 2,000-feature regression on 40 days of returns. Follow-up: "Where would ridge put the peak?" Code screen: `min_norm_test_error(p, seed)` reusing the generator stated above in the prelude (tests: the medians at p = 10, 40 and 400 over 20 seeds within 10%, computed with `budget` stated; the script times it; if it passes 1 s on desktop, test 5 seeds against the script's 5-seed medians instead).
- **l4-deep-learning-quickfire** (tags `dl-quickfire`; pace 60 s per question). Hook: ten quick questions against a `timed` feel (state the pace; lessons are not timed). Explore and ladder: the loss at initialisation should be ln V (4.1744 for 65 characters; a much larger value means a bug); He initialisation (0.0625 for fan-in 512); inverted dropout (×1.25 at p = 0.2) and nothing at test time; batch norm uses running statistics at test time, layer norm normalises per token and needs none; attention scores are divided by √d because their sd grows as √d (8 against 1.005). Trap: "dropout is applied at test time with the same mask" (`choice`). Edge: a batch of one with batch norm. Generalise: each fact as one spoken sentence and its reason. Transfer: a code review at a lab where the first loss printed is 9.7 for 65 classes. Follow-up: "Your loss starts at 0.01. What is wrong?" (`multi`: labels leak, a mask is missing, the loss is mean over the wrong axis). Code screen: `sanity_checks(logits, targets)` returning the expected and actual starting loss and a flag (tests: uniform logits give ln V exactly; a planted leak is flagged).
