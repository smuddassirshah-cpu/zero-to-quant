## The induction model (L2 and L4; also the base of `ai-safety` L3)

A two-layer, attention-only transformer with hand-built weights, so every head has a known job. The lessons say so plainly at l8: the weights were built to the circuit Olsson et al. found in trained models, and a trained model shows the same two heads less cleanly. The builder is `build(seed=0, noise=0.02)` in `docs/briefs/ai-36-scripts/induction.py`; the L2 agent ports it into its number script and writes `app/content/data/interp/induction-2l.json`.

- Tokens 0 to 9 and BOS = 10 (11 tokens). Context T = 17: BOS, then 8 distinct tokens, then the same 8 again. Residual width D = 50 in four slots: token one-hot (dims 0 to 10), position one-hot (11 to 27), previous-token slot (28 to 38), induction output slot (39 to 49). No layer norm, no MLP, no biases. Scores are divided by the square root of the head width; causal mask.
- L0H0, the previous-token head: query and key read the position slot, with the key shifted by one, so position i attends to i − 1 at score 8; its OV copies the attended token into the previous-token slot. L0H1, a decoy: zero query and key (uniform attention), writes 0.3 times the averaged token into the token slot. L1H0, the induction head: query reads the current token, key reads the previous-token slot (score 8 on a match), and BOS answers every query at score 4, so a query with no match rests on BOS, whose value is zero; its OV copies the attended token into the output slot. L1H1, a decoy: a duplicate-token head (score about 2 on the same token) that writes 0.1 times the token into the previous-token slot, which nothing later reads. The unembedding reads the output slot times 8.
- Every matrix then gets independent noise N(0, 0.02²) from `np.random.default_rng(0)`, in the order the builder lists. 11,900 numbers in all; at 6 significant figures the file is about 120 KB.
- File layout: `{"config": {"vocab": 11, "bos": 10, "T": 17, "D": 50, "slots": {...}}, "W_E": [11][50], "W_pos": [17][50], "W_U": [50][10], "layers": [[{"W_Q", "W_K", "W_V", "W_O"}, ...], ...]}`; `W_Q` and `W_K` are D by d_head (17 in layer 0, 11 in layer 1), `W_V` is D by 11, `W_O` is 11 by D.
- Evaluation sequences: 200 from `np.random.default_rng(1)`, each BOS plus 8 distinct tokens drawn with `rng.choice(10, 8, replace=False)`, then repeated. "First half" means the losses predicting positions 1 to 8; "second half" means the predictions made at positions 9 to 15 (the prediction at position 8 is unpredictable, since the token after it lies in the future).

Seeded figures (from `induction.py`; the second method is a per-position loop forward, which agrees with the vectorised forward to 6e-15 on the logits and gives the same second-half loss, 0.011010, on the first 20 sequences):
- Loss: first half 2.4448 nats (uniform guessing is ln 10 = 2.3026); second half 0.0104.
- Head scores, averaged over the 200 sequences (one method; the ablations below confirm each role): previous-token score (mean attention from i to i − 1) L0H0 0.9968, L0H1 0.1525, L1H0 0.0640, L1H1 0.0563; induction score (mean attention from i to i − 7 over positions 9 to 16) L1H0 0.8605, L1H1 0.0371, L0H1 0.0763, L0H0 0.0003.
- Zero ablation, second-half loss: L0H0 2.4034, L1H0 2.3530, L0H1 0.0046 (lower than the full model: the decoy was hurting), L1H1 0.0101. Mean ablation (the head's mean output over the 200 sequences and all positions): L0H0 2.3936, L1H0 2.3646.
- Activation patching, 100 pairs from `default_rng(7)`: clean BOS, a, a; corrupted BOS, b, a with b a permutation of a; prediction at position 15; logit difference = logit of the clean answer minus logit of the answer the corrupted context points to. Mean logit difference: clean 6.8453, corrupted −7.0393. Share recovered by patching one head's clean output at every position: L1H0 1.0000, L0H0 0.5083, L0H1 0.0035, L1H1 0.0000. The 0.51 is the lesson: with the previous-token head patched, the induction head looks in the right place but copies the corrupted token sitting there, so the difference lands near zero, halfway.
- Composition scores, ‖AB‖_F / (‖A‖_F ‖B‖_F) (from `compose.py`; second method an explicit triple loop for L0H0 to L1H0): K-composition L0H0 to L1H0 0.2678, L0H0 to L1H1 0.0200; L0H1 to L1H0 0.1246 and L0H1 to L1H1 0.2788 (the decoy writes into the token slot that every layer-1 query reads, so its Q-composition with L1H0 is 0.2836); the average for random 50 by 50 Gaussian matrices is 0.1414 (closed form 1/√50 = 0.1414).

- **l1-the-stream-as-a-sum.** Hook: three vectors in a 3-wide residual (an embedding (1, 0, 0), a head's write (0, 1, 0), an MLP's write (−1, 0, −1)); the learner fills a `table` with the stream after each and gets (0, 1, −1). Explore: every component reads the stream through its own layer norm and adds its output back; nothing is overwritten, so the final residual is the embedding plus every write. Ladder: (1) the three-vector sum; (2) the attention output split into heads: concat(z_h) W_O equals the sum over heads of z_h times its block of rows of W_O (a `table` with two heads of width 1); (3) on the tiny GPT, the final residual of one position equals embedding plus position plus four head writes per layer plus each MLP write. Trap: "each layer replaces the residual" (`choice`); "layer norm is linear, so it can be pushed through the sum" (it divides by a norm that depends on the whole sum). Edge: a head whose write at a position is nearly zero norm does nothing there; a `bars` of write norms shows it. Generalise: the paths view, where every later component can read any earlier write. Transfer: when a model misbehaves, the first thing to print is each component's write norm at the bad position. Code screens: `split_head_writes(z, W_O, n_heads)` returning a list of per-head writes (tests: shapes, and the sum equals `z @ W_O` to 1e-9 on supplied arrays); `stream_parts(cache, layer_count)` returning `(names, vectors)` in order (tests: names in order, the parts sum to the final residual to 1e-9, count 2 + n_layers × (n_heads + 1) when the token and position embeddings are counted apart, plus one bias part per attention layer if the `gpt` model has output biases).
- **l2-the-logit-lens.** Hook: a 3-wide stream and an unembedding W_U with rows (2, 0, −1), (0, 1, 1), (1, −1, 0) and tokens A, B, C; the learner unembeds the stream after the embedding, after the head and after the MLP: logits (2, 0, −1), (2, 1, 0), (−1, 2, 1); top token A, A, then B; the probability of B is 0.1142, 0.2447, then 0.7054 (second method: the closed form 1/(1 + e⁻³ + e⁻¹) = 0.7054 for the last). Explore: apply the final layer norm and W_U to the stream after each layer of the tiny GPT and plot the probability of the true next character by layer on `series`. Ladder: hand case; one position of the tiny GPT; the average over the 2,000 held-out characters. Trap: unembedding an intermediate residual without the final layer norm (the residual norm grows with depth, so late layers look overconfident; a `choice` with the two curves). Edge: if the `gpt` model ties `W_U` to `W_E`, the lens after the embedding predicts the input token itself; say which applies from the `gpt` brief. Generalise: the lens reads a prediction the model has not finished; the tuned lens (Belrose et al. 2023) learns one affine map per layer to correct the drift, read only. Transfer: a lens curve that is flat until the last layer means the work happens late. Code screens: `lens_logits(resid, ln_gain, ln_bias, W_U)` (tests: on the final residual it equals the model's logits to 1e-9; on the hand case it returns the three rows above); `lens_curve(cache, targets)` returning the mean probability of the target per layer (tests: length n_layers + 1, the last entry equals the model's mean probability, values in [0, 1]). Numbers for the tiny GPT come from the level's number script and are stated with their source.
- **l3-direct-logit-attribution.** Hook: in the hand case, which write raised B? Dot each part with B's column (0, 1, −1): embedding 0, head 1, MLP 1; total 2, the final logit of B. Explore: with a final layer norm the map is not linear, so freeze the norm's scale from the clean run and subtract each part's mean; then the parts add exactly to the logit, less the bias term. Ladder: one logit; a logit difference (B minus A: the parts give −2, 1 and 4, which add to 3, and the final logits give 2 − (−1) = 3); per head and per MLP on the tiny GPT for one position. Trap: attributing a raw logit instead of a difference (a constant added to every logit is no evidence; `choice`). Edge: a component with a large attribution whose removal changes little, because later layers compensate; this is why L4 intervenes (one sentence, pointing forward). Generalise: direct attribution measures only the direct path; indirect paths through later heads are not counted. Transfer: the first chart in most circuit papers is a direct attribution bar chart. Code screens: `direct_attribution(parts, scale, gamma, w_diff)` returning one number per part (tests: the sum equals the frozen-scale logit difference to 1e-9 on the tiny GPT; the hand case gives −2, 1, 4); `top_writers(parts_names, contribs, k)` (tests: order, ties broken by name).

Explore: over the 2,000 held-out characters, the share of positions where the lens top-1 after layer l equals the final top-1, and the mean direct attribution of each head and MLP to the correct character's logit difference against the runner-up. Ladder: one position, ten positions, all 2,000. Trap: reading one striking example as the rule (`choice`: the average and its spread). Edge: positions where the final prediction is wrong; the lens shows when the wrong answer took over. Generalise: what the lens and attribution cannot tell you (cause), which L4 answers. Transfer: a short write-up of what each layer adds, of the kind a research desk keeps. Code screens: `first_layer_agreeing(cache)` per position (tests on supplied caches: exact integers); `mean_attribution_table(caches)` returning a components-by-1 array (tests: shape, finite, and the sum over parts equals the mean frozen-scale logit difference to 1e-6). Trap: "the head looks at tokens" (it looks at positions; a `choice` with a shuffled-token sequence whose pattern does not change). Edge: position 1 attends to BOS, so BOS lands in the previous-token slot, and the induction head ignores it by design. Generalise: QK and OV are separate questions, so read them separately.
