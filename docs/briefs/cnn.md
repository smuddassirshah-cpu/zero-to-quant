- l1 (a rolling mean is a convolution): hook: the `numpy-pandas` l10 closes and their 3-day rolling mean; the learner writes the mean at one row as a dot product of three closes with $(\tfrac13, \tfrac13, \tfrac13)$, then slides it. Named: a 1D convolution slides a kernel along a signal and takes a dot product at each position; with "valid" positions only, $n$ inputs and a kernel of length $k$ give $n - k + 1$ outputs (10 for 12 closes and $k = 3$, matching the two NaNs of `rolling(3)`). Ladder: compute two outputs by hand; a weighted kernel (an exponentially weighted mean truncated to 5 weights, normalised to sum to 1); alignment: pandas puts the output on the window's last row, `np.convolve(..., "valid")` returns only full windows, and the learner matches the two. Trap: the flip. The mathematical convolution reverses the kernel, $(x * k)_t = \sum_j k_j x_{t-j}$, while deep learning libraries compute the unflipped sliding dot product (cross-correlation) and still call it convolution; a symmetric kernel hides the difference, $(1, 0, -1)$ shows it. Edge case: a kernel longer than the signal (no valid output). Generalise: convolution is linear and shift-equivariant: shifting the input shifts the output by the same amount (the learner shifts a spike and watches). `code` screens: `conv1d_valid(x, k)` by a loop (exact; tested against `np.correlate(x, k, "valid")`, and the test also checks the flip against `np.convolve`), `rolling_mean_as_conv(close, n)` returning a Series aligned like `rolling(n).mean()` (exact, NaNs included), `ewm_kernel(alpha, length)` (exact). Transfer: every moving-average signal on a desk is a convolution with a fixed kernel; a CNN learns the kernel instead of choosing it.
- l2 (kernels that detect): hook: three kernels and three outputs on a seeded price path; the learner matches $(1, -1)$ to the daily change, $(1, -2, 1)$ to the curvature, and a length-5 pattern kernel to a spike detector. Named: a kernel responds most where the signal locally looks like it (a sliding dot product is largest when the window lines up with the kernel; with both normalised it is a sliding cosine, `vectors` l3). Ladder: the difference kernel gives returns from prices (link `numpy-pandas` l9); the second difference; a matched kernel for a planted shape, and its peak at the shape's position; a threshold on the output to flag detections. Trap: reading a big output as "the pattern is here" when the signal is just large there (normalise the window first). Edge case: padding at the ends: "same" output with zeros added, and the false detections that zeros cause at the start of a series. `code` screens: `detect(x, k, threshold)` returning the positions where the normalised response exceeds the threshold (exact on a planted signal), `pad_same(x, k_len, mode)` for zero and edge padding (exact), `second_difference(x)` as a convolution (exact). Transfer: spotting a volume spike or a sharp reversal in a tick series with a fixed kernel, and why a learned kernel can find shapes no one named.
- l3 (convolution in two dimensions): hook: an 8 by 8 image of a seeded digit-like stroke; the learner slides a 3 by 3 kernel (vertical edge) across it on a `matrix` with frames and fills three output cells. Named: in 2D the kernel slides over rows and columns; an $n \times n$ image and a $k \times k$ kernel give $(n - k + 1) \times (n - k + 1)$ valid outputs (6 by 6 for 8 and 3). Ladder: a horizontal and a vertical edge kernel; their outputs on the same image (`pair` of `grid`s); several kernels give several feature maps (channels out); an input with several channels needs a kernel per channel, summed (one screen, shapes only). Trap: computing a matrix product of the image and the kernel instead of a sliding dot product. Edge case: a kernel of size 1 by 1 (a per-pixel scaling, which mixes channels and nothing else). `code` screens: `conv2d_valid(img, k)` by a double loop (exact; tested against a `scipy.signal.correlate2d` check only if you accept the heavy import, else against a hand case), `conv2d_multi(img, kernels)` returning a stack of feature maps (exact), `edge_map(img)` combining the two edge kernels into a gradient magnitude (exact). Transfer: an order book drawn as a 2D grid (price level by time) is an image; a 2D kernel looks for a local shape in it (keep it as one sentence: a plausible use, not a claim of edge).
- l4 (learning a kernel): hook: a seeded signal and its output under a hidden kernel of length 3, plus noise; the learner guesses the kernel from the picture, then lets gradient descent find it. Named: a kernel's weights are parameters like any others; the loss is the mean squared error between the convolution and the target, and its gradient with respect to each weight is a convolution of the input with the output error. Ladder: the gradient for one weight by hand on a 5-point signal; the conv op added to `cnn_ops.py` with its backward (input and weight gradients), gradient-checked to relative error 1e-6 in float64; gradient descent from zero recovers the planted kernel to within the noise (a `plane` of the loss against one weight with the others at their optimum). Trap: learning rate too large for a long signal (the loss scales with the number of positions; use the mean, not the sum). Edge case: a planted kernel longer than the learned one (the fit is the best short approximation, not the truth). `code` screens: `conv1d_backward(x, k, dout)` returning the gradients for `x` and `k` (exact; gradient check in the test), `learn_kernel(x, y, k_len, lr, steps)` returning the learned kernel within a `tol` of the planted one on the stated seed (within the budget rule), `kernel_gradient_check(x, k)` returning the largest relative error (below 1e-6).
