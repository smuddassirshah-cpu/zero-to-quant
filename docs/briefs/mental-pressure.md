# Level briefs: mental-pressure (Mental Maths Under Pressure, topic 3.3)

Course context. `mental-pressure` takes the Stage 0 `mental` course (topic 0.5, "Mental Arithmetic Drills": left to right, round and adjust, making 100, running totals, tables to 12, splitting, near-round numbers, squaring, division as a missing multiplication, decimal forms of fractions, percentage shortcuts, and l12 "Two minutes against the clock", whose Zetamac pacing lesson sets a target of 30 in 120 s, 4 s an answer) to interview speed (topic 3.3, track `speed`; `docs/curriculum.md` section 3.3: "Mental arithmetic: Zetamac at 50 or more on default settings, plus timed drills in the 80-questions-in-8-minutes format some firms use"; the topic and stage exit test both include "Zetamac at 50 or more"). The 80-in-8 format (associated with Optiver among others, as commonly described: say "some firms" and do not claim a firm's exact rules) is mixed arithmetic at 6 s a question: additions, subtractions, multiplications, divisions, decimals, fractions, percentages and the next term of a sequence; Zetamac at 50 is 2.4 s an answer. The course's strategies: left to right, complements, splitting and factor tricks, cross multiplication, known decimal forms, approximation when exactness is not asked; its pressure habits: error analysis after every set (which operation is slow, which is wrong, which errors are slips and which are method gaps), skip and return, the first-minute rule (settle into the pace in the first minute rather than racing it), a breath between sets, and checkpoints (10 a minute in the 80-in-8: 40 done at 4 minutes). What the app can and cannot time, read from the code, and binding on every level: the only clocked arithmetic in the app is the Today screen's daily warm-up (route `#warmup`, `app/js/screens/today.js`, settings in `app/content/warmup.json`): a 120-second round (and a 10-second demo that is not saved) on Zetamac's default settings (addition 2 to 100 plus 2 to 100, subtraction as addition reversed, multiplication 2 to 12 by 2 to 100, division as multiplication reversed, each operation equally likely, never the same problem twice running), integer answers typed into one field that moves on by itself when the answer is right (no Enter, no skip button, wrong answers simply wait), a timer pill that turns amber in the last 10 seconds, pause on quit, every full round saved with its score and logged to the `mental` hours block, and an end chart of the last 10 rounds with a dashed "Stage 3 mark" at 50 (`targets.stage3`; `targets.stage0` is 25); five rounds a day make the suggested 10 minutes. A lesson cannot launch the warm-up, change its settings, or read its scores, and no lesson may edit `warmup.json`; prose may tell the learner to open the daily warm-up from Today and to note their score. The only other clock is the exam timer (`content/exams/*.json` `minutes`, and a stage exam's `parts` each with its own clock), which belongs to exam authors, not to this course: a timed 80-question, 8-minute paper is a natural part for the topic 3.3 or Stage 3 exam, and each level agent should say so in the report rather than write an exam file. Lesson, review and homework screens have no countdown and grading never depends on time (`docs/ENGINE-BACKLOG.md` item 129, "a timed table set (countdown, live score, lock at zero)", is deferred and should be designed with this course: add a backlog bullet naming what you needed). So the drills in this course are `table` widgets of 10 to 20 editable cells (the form `mental/l12-against-the-clock.json` uses: one row a question, `answer` and `tol` per cell; table cells parse integers, decimals, fractions a/b, mixed numbers, percentages and thousands commas, and a cell with `unit: "%"` takes a percentage for a fractional answer), `numeric` items with `wrong[]` values keyed to the classic slips (for error analysis in `byKey`), and `choice` for "which method"; the prose gives a target time for the set ("20 questions at 6 s each: two minutes") that the learner keeps with their own watch; pace and error-analysis lessons use seeded logs of a made-up candidate's round (question, operation, seconds, right or wrong) generated in your scratchpad with `random.Random(seed)` and shown on a `table` or `bars` canvas, so every statistic the learner computes is exact. Use £ for money in word problems. Names to cite once per level: Zetamac (the curriculum lists `https://arithmetic.zetamac.com`; prose cannot hold links, so name it), and the green book and Heard on the Street only where a word problem resembles theirs (never quote them).

Shared runtime facts: Python runs in Pyodide 0.27.7 (NumPy and the rest exist, but this course's code items should be pure Python, so nothing slow loads); each test has about 5 s; no network. Canvases: `table` (question sets, logs, method cards), `bars` (seconds by operation, scores by day, errors by type), `number-line` (pace: seconds per answer against a target, as `mental` l12 did; checkpoints along an 8-minute line), `area` (splitting a product into a rectangle of parts, as `mental` level 2 did, for 2-digit by 2-digit), `grid` (a multiplication grid for cross multiplication), `series` (a score trend), `plane` (√x and its tangent for square-root estimates), `console` (a printed self-review).
