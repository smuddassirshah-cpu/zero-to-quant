# Level briefs: mm-games (Market-Making Games, topic 3.3)

Course context. `mm-games` trains the trader-interview games of topic 3.3, Interview preparation (track `speed`; `docs/curriculum.md` section 3.3: "Market-making games: quote a two-sided price on an unknown quantity and update it as trades come in. Practise aloud with a partner. Betting games: expected value, when to hedge, Kelly sizing", plus Jane Street's free Probability and Markets guide, "written by the firm about its own interviews", and mock interviews spoken aloud because interviewers mark the reasoning as it happens; the curriculum's Stage 2 track table says trader interviews test "probability, mental arithmetic, market-making and betting games"). Name the Jane Street guide once per level, with Zhou's green book and Crack's Heard on the Street for the estimation and betting questions, and never quote their text. The games this course must cover, mapped to its 13 lessons: making a market on an unknown quantity (l1, l4, l5: bid, ask, width and the fair value inside them); the dice and coin-flip betting games priced by expectation (l2, l6); width from uncertainty (l3); updating on trades (l7: being lifted or hit is information, and inventory skew, linking `micro` l10's rule m = V − γq); adverse selection in a game where the counterparty knows more (l8, linking `micro` l11's s = αδ); updating as information is revealed (l9); inventory under a position limit, the Kelly bet-sizing game and the expected value against variance trade-off under a bankroll (l10); hedging with a correlated instrument (l11); thinking aloud (l12); and a mock round (l13, and every level's homework), graded by numeric checks on the quotes in lessons and by a `longform` rubric in homework (the validator allows `longform` only in homework and exams, so no lesson may use it). The learner has `micro` (l1 bids and asks, l5 the cost of crossing the spread, l7 where is the price, l9 quoting both sides, l10 skewing for inventory with V = 500p, s = 2p, γ = 1p per lot, l11 adverse selection with α = 0.2 and δ = 10p giving s = 2p), `conditional` (Bayes, l9 natural frequencies), `random-vars` (l6 a fair price for a bet, l9 measuring spread), `markov` (l10 gambler's ruin), `fin-ml` l9 (the Kelly fraction `p − (1 − p)/b`), `mental` (Stage 0 speed), and, in the same topic, `puzzles` (l21 derives Kelly; this course plays it) and `mental-pressure`. Use £ for money throughout and pence only where the `micro` numbers are reused. A game is played in the app as a scripted sequence of screens: the "interviewer" is the prose, the learner's quote is a `table` widget or `sliders` (bid and ask) graded by tolerance against a band of acceptable quotes (for example a bid between fair − 2 sd and fair, an ask between fair and fair + 2 sd, a width no wider than a stated limit) via `sliders` `check` expressions, and each trade the interviewer makes is the next screen; there is no live opponent, no clock (lesson screens have none; `docs/ENGINE-BACKLOG.md` 129 is deferred), and every graded number is exact or has a stated tolerance. - l3 (how wide to quote): width from uncertainty: a quote fair ± k·sd catches a known share of outcomes; on the sum of two dice a 6 to 8 range holds 16/36 = 4/9 of outcomes, 5 to 9 holds 24/36 = 2/3, 4 to 10 holds 30/36 = 5/6 (a `bars` with the range shaded); for three dice 9 to 12 holds 104/216 = 13/27 = 0.4815; the trade-off (a tighter quote trades more and earns the spread more often, a wider quote loses less when you are wrong) on a `plane` toy where the interviewer trades whenever your price is through the realised value (expected loss to an informed interviewer against a stated earning from an uninformed one: use l8's numbers sparingly, here only the picture); the interviewer's rule in many games: "your width may be at most 2" (a constraint the learner must meet); trap: quoting the full range (1 to 6 on a die) when told to make a tight market, or a zero width on an uncertain quantity; edge case: a known quantity, zero width. `code` screen: `coverage(lo, hi, n_dice)` returning the share of outcomes in [lo, hi] (exact fractions). Transfer: a desk quoting wider before an earnings announcement, and why.
- l4 (Fermi estimates): break a quantity into factors you can guess, multiply, and keep track of the uncertainty of each factor; the interview questions where the answer is never checked: "how many litres of water in an Olympic pool" (50 m × 25 m × 2 m = 2,500 m³ = 2,500,000 litres: dimensions given, so exact), "how many trades does a market maker do in a day" (factors given in the prose as assumptions; grade the arithmetic of the product and the order of magnitude by `choice`); the error of a product: relative errors roughly add, in logs (a `table` of three factors each good to a factor of 2); round to one significant figure and work in powers of ten (link `mental-pressure` l7); trap: false precision (an answer of 2,437,000 trades from three guessed factors), or one wild factor hidden in a confident total; edge case: a factor that can be zero (a market closed on a holiday). `code` screen: `fermi_product(factors)` returning the product and the range from each factor's low and high (exact). Transfer: sizing a market before quoting on it: how many contracts might trade, so how wide and how large to quote.
