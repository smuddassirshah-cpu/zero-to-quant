### l13-rules-of-thumb: Rules of thumb for option prices (skill `tp-bs`)

Hook: "A one-year at-the-money option on a £100 share at 20% vol, no rates: what is it worth, without a calculator?" Method: the payoff near the money is max(S_T − S, 0), where S_T − S is about normal with standard deviation σ√T S, and the mean of max(Z, 0) is 1/√(2π) = 0.3989, so **C ≈ 0.4 σ√T S** (rounded). The plan's check: S = 100, σ = 0.2, T = 1, r = 0 gives 8.00 against Black-Scholes 7.9656 (rule too high by 0.43%); the exact form is S(2Φ(σ√T/2) − 1), and the 0.4 is that with Φ replaced by its slope at 0. Screens (10):
1. `explore`, `plane`: ATM call value against σ (0 to 0.6) for S = 100, r = 0, with `controls` stops on T (1, 0.25, 0.0625); only the exact curve shows. Question (choice): the vol doubles from 20% to 40% at T = 1; the price does what? Right: about doubles (£7.97 to £15.85). Slips: quadruples ("You squared the change, as variance does"); barely moves ("At the money the call is all time value, so it is the most sensitive to volatility").
2. `problem`, same plane at T = 0.25 patch: price with one year left is £7.97; predict it with three months left. numeric 4.00, tol 0.1 (BS 3.9878; ratio 0.5006, so "time quarters, price halves"). Keys: `quarter` 1.99 (price falls like time), `same` 7.97.
3. `ladder`, plane (`enter` draws the dashed rule line): name the rule, then use it: S = £50, σ = 32%, T = 0.25: numeric 3.20 tol 0.05 (BS 3.1881). Keys: `no-root` 1.60 (used T, not √T), `whole-number-vol` 320 (typed 32, not 0.32).
4. `derive`, `order`: four lines with a reason each: payoff and its spread σ√T S; the mean of max(Z, 0) is 0.3989; so C ≈ 0.3989 σ√T S with nothing to discount at r = 0; round to 0.4. One distractor line ("the mean of max(Z, 0) is 0.5", `why`: that is P(Z > 0), not the mean of max(Z, 0)). Script check: ∫₀^∞ z φ(z) dz = 0.398942.
5. `ladder`, plane: the rule is a straight line in σ with slope 0.4√T S = 40 per 1.00 of vol, that is 0.40 a vol point; vega ≈ price ÷ σ (39.83 against the true 39.695). A 2 point rise (20% to 22%) adds: numeric 0.80 tol 0.05 (BS gain 0.7935). Keys: `percent-slip` 80 (used 2, not 0.02 as units), `half` 0.40.
6. `trap`, plane at the r = 5% option: the rule says 8.00; the market and Black-Scholes say 10.45. Question (choice): why does the rule fail here? 8. `ladder`, plane (edge case): the strike moves away from the money (r = 0): 5%, 10%, 20% out of the money are worth £5.91, £4.29, £2.15 against the rule's £8.00. numeric (read from the curve) 4.29 tol 0.1 for K = 110. Takeaway: the rule is for the money only.

### l16-the-smile: The volatility smile (skill `tp-bs`)

Hook: "Both puts and calls on the same expiry: why is the 90 strike quoted at a higher vol than the 110?" Model for numbers: S = 100, T = 1, r = 0, the lesson's equity skew σ(K) = 0.20 − 0.002(K − 100) (22% at 90, 18% at 110, valid on 70 to 130; the script checks the implied density is positive there). Key results: the risk-neutral chance of finishing above K is the digital call −∂C/∂K taken **along the smile**, = e^{−rT}Φ(d₂) − vega·σ′(K), here with σ′ = −0.002; the second term is the skew's correction. Table (flat 20% against the skew): P(S_T > 100) 0.4602 against **0.5396** (the correction is vega 39.695 × 0.002 = 0.0794); P(S_T > 110) 0.2821 against 0.3402; P(S_T < 70) 0.0462 against **0.0813** (1.76 times); P(S_T > 130) 0.0790 against 0.0416; P(S_T < 80) 0.1549 against 0.1630.
