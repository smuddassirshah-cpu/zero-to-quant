The pure-course rule. A free-form proof cannot be graded. Proof is taught by guided steps: `order` (arrange the lines of a proof), `choice` and `multi` (pick the justification for a step, spot the flawed line), fill-in widgets (`numeric`, `expression`, `table`) for the computed parts, `tap-regions` on a `table` of proof lines (`tap: "rows"`) to spot the line that fails, `place` for building a statement from chips, and `longform` homework with a 10-point rubric and a full model answer for whole proofs. Each lesson paragraph below says which widget grades each step of each proof taught; follow it or say in your report why you changed it. `code` screens are optional in this course. Use one only where the brief names it, where it tests something real (checking a conjecture on many cases before proving it, searching for a counterexample), with hidden tests the starter fails, and say on the screen that checking cases is evidence, not proof.

Notation for the whole course (match it; later courses will). Words first, then the symbol, introduced in the lesson that names it: $P \land Q$ (and), $P \lor Q$ (or, inclusive), $\lnot P$ (not), $P \Rightarrow Q$ (if $P$ then $Q$), $P \Leftrightarrow Q$ (if and only if), $\forall$ and $\exists$. Truth values T and F in tables. Sets: $\{1, 2, 3\}$, $\{n \in \mathbb{Z} : n \text{ is even}\}$, $\in$, $\notin$, $\subseteq$, $\varnothing$, $|A|$, $A \cup B$, $A \cap B$, $A \setminus B$, $A^c$ (as `counting-chance` l3 already writes it), $A \times B$, $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$. Functions: $f : A \to B$, domain, codomain, image; `functions` l3 called the set of outputs the "range", so say once in l8 that range and image mean the same set and the codomain is the declared target. Even means $n = 2k$ for some integer $k$; odd means $n = 2k + 1$. End a proof with "as required" or $\blacksquare$, consistently. Sequences use $u_n$ as `series` does; the limit definition in l5 uses $u_n$ and $L$.
