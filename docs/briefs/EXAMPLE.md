- l3 (testing for a unit root): the random walk x_t = x_{t−1} + ε_t against the AR(1) x_t = φ x_{t−1} + ε_t with |φ| < 1; the test regression Δx_t = (φ − 1) x_{t−1} + ε_t, so the null is φ = 1, i.e. the coefficient on x_{t−1} is 0; why the usual t statistic does not follow a t distribution under the null (the Dickey-Fuller distribution is shifted left; a seeded simulation on `distribution` or `bars` of 2,000 random walks' t statistics against the normal; the learner reads that −1.65 is nowhere near the 5% point); the critical values (about −2.86 at 5% with a constant, −3.41 with a trend, for large n; cite Hyndman or MacKinnon in one sentence); the augmented version adds lagged differences to mop up autocorrelation (`adfuller(x, autolag="AIC")`); reading the result: statistic, p-value, lags used, critical values (a `number-line` with the statistic and the three critical values, the learner taps whether to reject); the test on a seeded random walk (fail to reject), on a seeded AR(1) with φ = 0.8 (reject), on prices (fail), on returns (reject strongly); low power against φ = 0.98 with 250 days (a `plane` of rejection rate against φ from a seeded simulation; the learner reads the rate at 0.98); the KPSS test as the mirror (null of stationarity) in one screen. Trap: reading a non-rejection as proof of a unit root (low power); using the normal critical value; including a trend term when there is none (and the reverse). - l4 (differencing a trend away): the first difference of a random walk is white noise; of a random walk with drift is noise around a constant; of a deterministic trend plus noise is also stationary but over-differenced (an MA(1) with a unit root in the MA part; say what that costs in one sentence); the `series` of a trending series and its difference with a slider on the drift; integrated of order d, I(1) and I(2) (a series that needs differencing twice: a `series` trio); seasonal differencing in one screen (a 5-day pattern in volume); log then difference gives returns (link to l1); when to difference and when to detrend (stochastic against deterministic trend: the ADF with a trend term decides); the price of differencing: memory lost (link to fin-ml l6's fractional differencing if it exists; one sentence).
