# Level briefs: vector-calc (Vector Calculus, topic 1.11)

Course context. `vector-calc` is the pure-maths course of topic 1.11 (30 h, 3 levels of 4 lessons, `"strand": "pure"`, art `grid`). It is not needed to be hired; it rounds out a maths degree and feeds physics-style models (the heat equation in `pdes` l2 builds on `l5-divergence-and-curl`). The cover line in `docs/curriculum.md` section 1.11 is "line integrals, surface integrals, Green's theorem, Stokes' theorem, the divergence theorem"; the reference is the second half of MIT 18.02 (on OCW Scholar, 18.02SC: vector fields, line integrals and Green's theorem in Unit 3, flux, the divergence theorem and Stokes' theorem in Unit 4), and 18.02 problem sets set the homework's level. The plan is the `### vector-calc` section of `docs/briefs/breadth-plan.md`. The learner has all of `multivar` (partial derivatives $f_x$, $f_y$, Clairaut's $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$ in l3, the chain rule in l4, the gradient $\nabla f = (f_x, f_y)$ as the steepest-climb arrow at right angles to contours in l5 and l6, double integrals $\iint_R f\,dA$ in l13 and l14, polar $dA = r\,dr\,d\theta$ in l15, the Jacobian $|J|$ in l17, triple integrals $\iiint_E f\,dV$ with cylindrical $r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz$ and spherical $\rho^2\sin\phi$, $\phi$ measured down from the $z$-axis, in l18; the minimum variance portfolio in l12 with $\sigma_1 = 0.2$, $\sigma_2 = 0.1$, $\rho = 0.3$), `vectors` (bold lower-case vectors, the dot product, lengths and angles) and `integrals` (the fundamental theorem, $\left[F\right]_a^b$ in l7). **No lesson in the app teaches the cross product**: level 3 l9 introduces it.

Shared rules for all three levels:

- **Notation.** A field is $\mathbf F = (P, Q)$ in the plane and $\mathbf F = (P, Q, R)$ in space; say once in l1 that this bold capital is a field, not a matrix (AUTHORING section 6 reserves bold capitals for matrices; note the exception in the report). A curve is $\mathbf r(t)$, $a \le t \le b$; $ds = |\mathbf r'(t)|\,dt$; $d\mathbf r = \mathbf r'(t)\,dt$; $\oint_C$ for a closed curve, counterclockwise unless stated; $\mathbf n$ is the outward (or chosen) unit normal; $dS$ the area element; $\oiint_S$ for a closed surface (KaTeX 0.16.11 in `app/vendor/katex` renders `\oiint`). Divergence is $\nabla \cdot \mathbf F$; in the plane the scalar curl is $Q_x - P_y$, written $\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F$; in space $\nabla \times \mathbf F$ (the determinant form only after l9 has taught the cross product). Partial derivatives as subscripts, as `multivar` does. Spherical coordinates as `multivar` l18: $\rho$, $\phi$ from the $z$-axis, $\theta$ round it.
- **Pure-course grading.** A free-form proof is not gradable. Theorems (the fundamental theorem for line integrals, Green, the divergence theorem, Stokes) are taught by guided steps: `order` (arrange the lines of a derivation, each line carrying its justification), `choice` or `multi` (pick the justification for a step, spot the flawed line, pick the right parametrisation or orientation), `numeric` for a computed integral, `expression` for a curl, divergence, potential or parametrisation component, `table` for a working grid (four sides of a square, six faces of a box), and `drag-point` or `slider` on `plane` where they fit. Each lesson paragraph below says which widget grades each step. Homework adds a `longform` item with a 10-point rubric and a full model answer.
- **Widget facts that matter here.** `expression` grades one scalar: a vector answer (a 3D curl, a cross product, a gradient) is graded component by component, either as one `expression` per screen or as a `table` of numeric values at a stated point. Never put a capital `N` or `D` in an expression answer (the maths engine reads them as its own functions; the validator warns) and do not use `e` or `i` as variable names (`e` is Euler's number). Use `upToConstant: true` for a potential function. For an exact answer with $\pi$ ($2\pi$, $\tfrac{3\pi}{8}$) prefer `expression` (constants compare numerically); for a decimal use `numeric` with `tol: { "abs": 0.001 }` and say "to 3 decimal places" in the question.
- **Code screens.** Only where a numeric integral genuinely tests something: the speed factor in a scalar line integral, the dot product in work, both sides of a theorem. They use SciPy (`scipy.integrate.quad`, `dblquad`, `tplquad`) in Pyodide 0.27.7; the first SciPy load takes several seconds (measure it in the playthrough and state it in the `how` of every screen that imports it; the widget gives heavy imports an extra 30 s). `dblquad(func, a, b, gfun, hfun)` integrates `func(y, x)` with the inner variable first: say so in the starter's docstring, because it is the trap that breaks most first attempts. The starter is a stub that fails every hidden test; every `solution` passes them; tests use `compare: "isclose"` with `tol: { "abs": 1e-6 }`. Functions take Python callables (`lambda p: p[0] + p[1]`), so tests pass lambdas built with `math`.
- **Engine limits known before writing** (put each one you hit in `docs/BACKLOG-INBOX.md`): (1) `plane` has no vector-field layer: arrows are hand-listed `state.vectors` entries `{ id, v: [dx, dy], from: [x, y] }`, so generate each field's arrow list (a 5 by 5 or 7 by 7 lattice, lengths scaled to fit) with a script in your scratchpad, and a field that changes with a slider needs precomputed groups switched with `hide`; wanted: `field: { P: "-y", Q: "x", grid: 7, scale }` on `plane`. (2) There is no 3D canvas (`docs/ENGINE-BACKLOG.md` item 241, deferred): level 3 draws surfaces as fixed oblique projections, parametric curves on `plane` with `axes: false`, and draws $\mathbf r_u$, $\mathbf r_v$ and their parallelogram on `vectors`. (3) `expression` is scalar only, as above.
- **Quant thread for transfer screens** (reuse these numbers so the course hangs together): a strategy's P&L along a price path is the line integral $\int \mathbf h \cdot d\mathbf S$ of its holdings field $\mathbf h$ (l3); holdings that are a gradient give a path-independent P&L, and a triangular currency loop with a non-zero total is an arbitrage (l4); round-trip P&L over a closed price loop is the curl of the holdings times the enclosed area (l6, l12); a market maker's buy-low-sell-high loop in the (inventory, price) plane earns its enclosed area (l7); drift fields of mean-reverting factor models have negative divergence (l5, l8, l11); a uniform random stress direction in three factors puts $\tfrac13$ of the squared move on each (l10). Money is £ throughout.
