## Seed and dataset conventions

- Random draws: `np.random.default_rng(seed)`, one call `standard_normal((n, 2))` (or `(n_paths, n_days, 2)` in the homework), rows are draws and `Z @ L.T` applies the factor. Seeds: 42 (first three draws), 7 and 11 (sample covariance tests), 2024 (the VaR figure), 3, 5, 7, 9 (homework).
- Scatter clouds: `sim` with `seed` 3, 4 and 9 in l15 and l16; the l16 hook cloud and the $Y = SX$ trap cloud are explicit points from `default_rng(5)` and `default_rng(8)`.
- Money: £ throughout; £1m positions and 1% = £10,000 per £1m.
- Column convention for the generator and the credit matrix, as in l3 and l4. Markov Chains uses rows; a note in l14's transfer says only that $e^{\mathbf{Q}}$ is a yearly Markov matrix like l3's.
- Tolerances: arrays compared with `np.round(..., 4).tolist()` and `compare: "isclose"`; sample covariances with a stated per-case `tol`; the shortest-fit tests use `rcond`/`tol` values quoted in each `how`.

## Notes and deviations from the brief

- l13's transfer reports eigenvalues and the trace, and warns that for non-orthogonal portfolios $\mathbf{M}^T\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{M}$ is a congruence, not a similarity (trace 16, not 7). The brief said "total variance as the trace for an orthogonal $\mathbf{M}$"; I added the contrast.
- l14: the hook is an explore with a time slider and two curves, as briefed. An earlier draft of its feedback said the peak of $u_2$ was about 0.16; the exact peak is 0.1925, now stated as 0.19.
- l15 uses `cholesky_2x2` with no NumPy (fast) and `correlated_draws` with NumPy. Both a ladder screen on triangular solves and the pivot screen were kept; the 20%/30% example and the VaR transfer are as briefed. The suspect matrix and the semidefinite case sit in the quiz.
- l16: the explore shows the population line $y = \rho x$ (a `derive` on `line.m`), not the sample `fit`, so the slope is exactly $\rho$ and the numeric answer 1.2 is exact. The tail $e^{-d^2/2}$ is stated as a fact proved in topic 1.5.
- l17: the near-copy uses $\delta = 10^{-6}$ rather than $10^{-12}$. NumPy's default `pinv` cut-off ($\approx 7 \times 10^{-16}$) would not drop a ratio of $10^{-13}$ either, so the brief's remark that `rcond` sets it to zero holds only when `rcond` is raised; the lesson teaches an explicit `tol` (relative to $\sigma_1$).
- Hidden tests in l17 and `h-code-lstsq` check that the source does not call `np.linalg.pinv` or `lstsq`, inside the same case as a value check (so the untouched starter fails it), and say so in `how`.
