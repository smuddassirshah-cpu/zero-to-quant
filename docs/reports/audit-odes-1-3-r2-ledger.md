# Claim ledger: odes L1 to L3 (concept audit, round 2)

Blind round-2 audit of `app/content/courses/odes/` levels 1 to 3 (l1 to l13, review-1 to review-3, homework-1 to homework-3), read after the round-1 fix pass was merged and before any earlier review, audit, ledger or script for this course was opened. One row per definition, result, method step, convention, always/never/every/only claim, interpretation, causal claim, code-behaviour claim and the reasoning of each explain, why, hint and feedback line (lines that restate the same reasoning share a row). Paths are JSON paths inside the file. Verdicts: correct, wrong, imprecise, misleading, unverified. `sim:` names a check in `docs/reports/odes-L<n>-concepts-r2.py`. A verdict of `imprecise` or `wrong` carries the finding number in `docs/reviews/audit-odes-1-3-r2.md`.

Sources used: Boyce and DiPrima, *Elementary Differential Equations and Boundary Value Problems* (B&D): sec. 1.3 (order, linearity), 2.1 (integrating factors), 2.2 (separable equations), 2.4 (Theorems 2.4.1 and 2.4.2, existence and uniqueness, interval of validity), 2.5 (autonomous equations, phase line, semistable solutions), 3.1 to 3.4 (characteristic equation, Wronskian, complex and repeated roots), 3.5 (undetermined coefficients and its modification rule), 3.7 and 3.8 (damping, forced vibrations, resonance), 7.1, 7.5, 7.6, 7.8 (systems, eigenvector solutions, complex and repeated eigenvalues), 9.1 (phase portraits of linear systems), 9.2 (definitions of stable, asymptotically stable, unstable), 9.3 (locally linear systems, Table 9.3.1), 9.4 and 9.5 (competing species, predator and prey), 10.1 (two-point boundary value problems). Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*: sec. 2.2 to 2.4 (fixed points, linear stability, half-stable points), 5.1 and 5.2 (classification, the trace-determinant plane, borderline cases), 6.3 (linearisation, hyperbolic fixed points, Hartman-Grobman), 6.4 (competition), 6.5 (conservative systems). Tenenbaum and Pollard, *Ordinary Differential Equations*: lessons 6, 11, 20, 21, 28. Ogata, *Modern Control Engineering*, sec. 5.3 (second-order step response, overshoot, peak time, settling). Vasicek (1977) for the mean-reverting drift; Hull, *Options, Futures and Other Derivatives*, ch. 4 (continuous compounding) and ch. 31 (Vasicek); Merton (1973) and Shreve, *Stochastic Calculus for Finance II*, sec. 8.3, for the perpetual American put. Code-behaviour claims were run in CPython with NumPy and, where the claim is about the learner's runtime, in Pyodide 0.27.7 under node.
