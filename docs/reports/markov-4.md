## Departures from the brief

- Every number the brief quoted was recomputed and is correct (inventory $\pi$, venue $\pi$, 336 and 168, the grid posterior, 0.1353, 0.1606, $\tfrac35$, 0.0025, 0.216). None was infeasible. Three teaching points needed a change:
  - **Burn-in picture.** The brief asked for a seeded run's visit shares to show that a chain started at 0.40 forgets its start. On a seven-state grid the chain forgets within about 18 steps, so a seeded run's shares at 50 steps already match the target (seed 1 has 0.04 at 0.40). l15 instead draws the exact distribution after $k$ steps from `matpow(P, k)` with a slider, so the early bias is visible and exact, and the seeded run appears in the transfer screen.
  - **Ergodic theorem.** The brief said the theorem extends the strong law to "an irreducible, aperiodic chain's time averages". Time averages need only irreducibility (lesson 6's swap chain has settling time shares). l15 says so and lists aperiodicity as the condition for $P^k$ to converge, with a distractor for it.
  - **Conditional uniformity.** The brief put it in the l16 ladder. The lesson had no room (11 screens), so it is a quiz item (exactly one trade in 10 minutes, chance it came in the first 2 minutes is 0.2) and a homework item (the first of three arrivals before time 1, $\tfrac{19}{27}$).
- The l14 edge case of a network in two pieces is a quiz item, and l16's changing-rate edge case is a quiz item and a summary line, to keep each lesson to 12 screens or fewer.
- Homework `order` item is the proof that the time reversal has $P^*_{ji} = \pi_iP_{ij}/\pi_j$ (reversible exactly when $P^* = P$). The two proofs the brief suggested (balance implies stationary, Metropolis satisfies balance) are the `derive` screens of l13 and l15, so the homework adds a new proof rather than repeating them.
- The longform critique uses the brief's scenario with concrete numbers (20 chains of 200 steps from 0.40, rejected steps dropped, reported mean 0.572). Dropping rejections changes the mean only by 0.0016 (to 0.5764), so the answer says the main damage is to the tails and to $P(p > 0.5)$, and that the burn-in bias (0.0059) is the larger error on the mean.
- Dependencies: `limits-thm` level 4 is not written. l16 states that the third arrival's wait is a Gamma with shape 3 and rate 2 and cites "the limits course" without a lesson number. l15 uses a grid posterior in place of the Beta, so it needs no limits-thm fact.
