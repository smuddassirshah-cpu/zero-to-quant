# Claim ledger: odes L1 to L3 (concept audit)

Blind audit of `app/content/courses/odes/` levels 1 to 3 (l1 to l13, review-1 to review-3, homework-1 to homework-3). One row per definition, result, method step, convention, always/never/every/only claim, interpretation, causal claim, code-behaviour claim and the reasoning of each explain, why, hint and feedback line (lines that restate the same reasoning share a row). Paths are JSON paths inside the file. Verdicts: `correct`, `wrong`, `imprecise`, `misleading`, `unverified`. `sim:` names a check in `docs/reports/odes-L<n>-concepts.py`.

Sources used: Boyce and DiPrima, *Elementary Differential Equations and Boundary Value Problems* (BDP), sec. 1.3 (order, linearity), 2.1 (integrating factors), 2.2 (separable), 2.4 (Theorems 2.4.1 and 2.4.2, existence and uniqueness, interval of validity), 2.5 (autonomous equations, phase line, semistable points), 3.1 to 3.4 (characteristic equation, Wronskian, Theorem 3.2.1, complex and repeated roots), 3.5 (undetermined coefficients, Table 3.5.1), 3.7 and 3.8 (damping, amplitude R, forced response and its maximum), 7.5 to 7.8 (linear systems, defective matrices), 9.1 to 9.3 (phase portraits, almost linear systems, Theorem 9.3.2 and Table 9.3.1); Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*, sec. 2.2 to 2.5 (flows on the line, linear stability, uniqueness), 5.1 and 5.2 (definitions of stability, classification, Fig. 5.2.8), 6.1 to 6.4 (phase portraits, no crossing, linearisation and its borderline cases, Lotka-Volterra); Tenenbaum and Pollard, *Ordinary Differential Equations*, Lessons 6, 11, 20 to 22, 28, 58; Ogata, *Modern Control Engineering*, sec. 5.3 (step response, overshoot); Hull, *Options, Futures and Other Derivatives*, ch. 4 (continuous compounding) and ch. 31 (Vasicek); Merton (1973) for the perpetual American put. Short derivations are given inline.
