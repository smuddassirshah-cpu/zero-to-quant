## Level 1

1. l4-tests-in-words.json | `screens[5].explain.text` | imprecise | "t = 1 x sqrt(4) = 2, so p ≈ 0.0455, a borderline result." The screen builds t from four yearly returns ("yearly returns are close to independent", t = SR x sqrt(years)), so the reference distribution is t with 3 degrees of freedom and the two-sided tail is 0.139, not 0.0455. The normal figure is right only when the Sharpe ratio is estimated from many daily or monthly returns, which the text never says. | Basis: t_3 tail 2 x P(T_3 > 2) = 0.139 (scipy); Monte Carlo under the null with four yearly returns rejects at abs(t) >= 2 about 13.9% of the time (`L1:l4 s5`, the one check in the L1 script that fails by design); Lo (2002) for the Sharpe standard error. | Fix, `screens[5].explain.text`: "$t = 1 \times \sqrt{4} = 2$. With the Sharpe ratio estimated from daily returns, as it usually is, the normal curve applies and $p \approx 0.0455$, a borderline result; from four yearly figures alone the $t_3$ curve gives $p \approx 0.14$. Halving the Sharpe ratio needs four times the years for the same $t$." Then make the L1 concepts script's check read the daily-return hypothesis (the fixer edits the final assert of the `l4 s5` block).

2. l4-tests-in-words.json | `screens[10].widget.options[3].why` | misleading | "A $t$ statistic of 3 is not a Sharpe ratio of 3, and luck explains it here." The second clause says the null is true. The item says "its slope", so the key is right, but the generic line is what a learner carries to a multiple regression. | Basis: SE = sigma-hat/sqrt(S_xx) with sigma-hat^2 = RSS/(n - k) (Wooldridge 2.5, 3.4); `L1:l1 quiz1`. | Fix, `quiz[1].feedback.generic`: "Not quite right. ## Level 2

4. l6-bias-variance-aloud.json | `screens[6].prose` | imprecise | "$E[(\hat{y} - y)^2] = \text{bias}^2 + \text{variance} + \text{noise}$" is given as the rule with no loss and no expectation named. The decomposition needs squared loss and an expectation over training samples (and the new noise) at a fixed x; an interviewer asking "expectation over what?" is the standard follow-up, and the lesson's own s3 took that expectation (400 refits, averaged over a grid) without saying so in the rule. | Basis: ESL 7.3 eq 7.9; the decomposition fails for absolute or 0-1 loss (ESL 7.3.1). | Fix, `screens[6].prose`, replace "What you found, as the rule:" with "What you found, as the rule, for squared loss and averaged over training samples at one $x$:" and in the spoken line replace "Expected error is bias squared plus variance plus noise." with "Expected squared error, over the samples we might have fitted, is bias squared plus variance plus noise."

5. l7-ridge-against-lasso.json | `screens[5].prose` (and `summary.formula.note`) | imprecise | "$\hat\beta^{\text{lasso}} = \text{sign}(b)\max(|b| - \lambda, 0)$" with "uncorrelated, standardised columns" names no scaling of the squared error. The threshold equals lambda only when the squared error is divided by 2n (scikit-learn's Lasso, which the lesson's seeded runs use); with the loss RSS + lambda L1 the threshold is lambda/(2n). The summary note states the ridge scaling (alpha = n lambda) but not the lasso one, so the two formulas sit on different unstated conventions. | Basis: ESL 3.4.3 Table 3.4 gives soft-thresholding for the loss (1/2) RSS + lambda L1 on orthonormal X; `L2:l7 s0, s1, s5` reproduces both formulas with `Lasso(alpha=lambda)` and `Ridge(alpha=n*lambda)` on X^T X = nI. | Fix, `summary.formula.note`: "With $X^\top X = nI$ and the squared error divided by $2n$, as scikit-learn's Lasso scales it; ridge with $\alpha = n\lambda$ gives $b/(1 + \lambda)$."
