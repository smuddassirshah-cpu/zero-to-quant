# Concept audit: conditional (topic 1.5), levels 4 and 5, round 1

Scope: `app/content/courses/conditional/` l18 to l26, review-4, review-5, homework-4 (with its performance task `h-host-rules-code`), homework-5, problems-4, problems-5. Blind read: no earlier review, audit, ledger, concepts script or author report for this course was opened until the ledger, the findings below and the script were committed. Method. Every puzzle was solved under the protocol the item states and under each protocol it does not rule out. A graded key that changes under an unstated protocol is a finding. Every explanation was checked as an argument, not only for its number. Code was run: solution against hidden tests, starter against the same tests, seeded outputs against the stated values.

Format: `N. file | JSON path | severity | problem | evidence and basis | exact fix`.

## Checked and clean

- Monty Hall. The tree, the Bayes derivation, the random host (1/2), the lazy host (1/2 and 1), four doors (3/8 and 3/4), 100 doors (99/100) and the 1/(1+q) formula are right. The unconditional arguments ("switching wins whenever the first pick was wrong") are valid for any tie-break. The performance task's three hosts, eleven tests and nine test-specific feedback lines were hand-checked and run.
- Bertrand's box. Coin counting, the tree, the odds form, the second GS box, the share-class transfer and the homework second draw are right. problems-4 `p-marble-bags` and `p-dated-coins` state the dealer's and assistant's rules explicitly and are right.
- Two children and Tuesday boy. The counts (7, 27, 13), the formula (2k − 1)/(4k − 1), the limit 1/2, `q-which-son` (1/3) and the homework long-form (13/27, 1/2, 1/3) are right. problems-4 `p-two-flags` and `p-two-strategies` state their protocols and are right.
- Gambler's fallacy. Independence is assumed where it is stated ("fair coin", "one day's move told you nothing about the next"); dependence is taught where it exists (cards without replacement, unknown coin, mean-reverting spread). Doubling has expected profit zero for a fair bet and −£0.331 for the 0.45 bet. The seeded 0.513 in homework-5 was reproduced.
- Prosecutor's fallacy. Prior and pool size are both taught: 1/6 in a city, 0.99999 among ten suspects, 2/3 with a better test, and the defence's mirror-image fallacy. Expected false discoveries use linearity, so the dependence between strategies tested on one price history does not affect the keyed counts.
- Simpson's paradox. All tables, the crossover at 3/7 and the weighted-average derivation are right. Each split in the lesson is by a variable fixed before the choice, so each keyed conclusion is right. review-5 `r-model-error` "must be true": a reversal forces both models to have the same harder group (if they disagreed, A overall ≤ A's worse rate < B's better rate ≤ B overall), and then A must weight it more.
- Survivorship. All averages and the gap formula are right; random closure is unbiased (Monte Carlo 2.385 against 2.4); the index backtest contrasts original members with today's members correctly.
- Trading signal. Posteriors, the 80% threshold, the copied signal, Kelly at even money (grid search confirms 2p − 1 maximises expected log growth), and the in-sample winner regressing out of sample (Monte Carlo 0.580 in sample, 0.550 on new data). The lesson has no code, so there is no look-ahead to check; "hit rate" is defined on the screen as the share of flashes that were right, and is kept apart from the detection rate.
