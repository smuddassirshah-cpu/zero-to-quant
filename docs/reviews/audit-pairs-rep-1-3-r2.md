## Checked and clean

The design and the trading rule are stated as the paper states them: 12-month formation and 6-month trading with monthly starts and six overlapping portfolios; the cumulative total-return index normalised to 1; shares with a non-trading day screened out; each share matched to its nearest partner by SSD and the top 5 and top 20 traded, with the course's "closest pairs overall" and "one pair per share" presented as its own variants and never as the paper's rule; the trigger at two historical standard deviations from the formation period; closing at the next crossing or the last day of the period, reopening allowed; the delisting rule (the paper's "delisting return, or the last available price" is given as the last price, which coincides for the cash takeover the screen poses); $1 long and $1 short; committed against employed capital with the 0/0 rule stated as the course's; the one-day wait as the paper's answer to the bid-ask bounce; "up to 11%" annualised; profits surviving conservative cost estimates; lower profits in later years; factor exposures small and a common factor in pairs returns. Conventions stated once where they first matter: `np.std` with n, six-month return over 6 as the monthly figure, compounding to annualise, no re-basing (see finding 1), £1 a side. The statistical reasoning holds: overlap inflates the naive t and Newey-West with 5 lags is the right lag (reproduced: 13.55 to 8.09, 11.09 to 6.91; under H0 the naive t rejects 42% at nominal 5%, NW(5) 13%); the bounce is captured by a same-day fill and not by a next-day fill (fresh panels: gap 2.5 points a cycle same day, 0.1 with the wait); random pairs on mids average 0 within noise on fresh panels and the bounce lifts them on closes; forced-close losses come 90% from chance or broken pairs; the crossing share separates faster reversion (0.91) from a break (0.50); Engle-Granger on one year with a bounce has size about 8 to 10% and power about 72% on the planted pairs, and shortlisting on closeness lifts the chance-pair pass rate to 27 to 37%; the best of 12 noisy cells keeps 68% of its in-sample figure out of sample with no true decline; a hedge of 2 or 0.5 sits outside the distance top 20 in 100% and 96% of constructed years; the market cancels in a £1 against £1 pair up to second order (beta 0.053, R^2 0.02 reproduced). Every code widget's solution passes its hidden tests and every starter fails (29 widgets); no look-ahead or whole-sample statistic reaches a threshold.

Unverified (not findings): l4 screens[2].explain and l8 screens[3] on the paper compounding daily returns within each month and averaging the six live portfolios; l11 screens[5].explain and homework-3 items[6].model attributing Do and Faff's (2010) explanation of the decline to fewer pairs converging. Both are consistent with every secondary description reached and should be read against the two papers.
