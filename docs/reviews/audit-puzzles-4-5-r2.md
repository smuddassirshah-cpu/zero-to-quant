## Level 4

1. l22-when-to-hedge.json | `.screens[2].hints[0].text` | imprecise | The hint says "At fair odds a node is worth the average of its two children." Elsewhere in this lesson "fair odds" means odds that match the true chance (screens[4], quiz[0]: "a hedge at fair odds changes the variance, not the expectation"), so a learner carries away that the £31.25 series bet needs each game to be 50-50. It does not: the bets copy the series payoff whatever the true chance, because the market offers evens. The prose, the generalise screen and the summary say "at evens"; only this hint says "fair odds". | Replication argument: with bets at evens the strategy "bet half the gap" ends at exactly +£100 or −£100 on every path, so the node value is the average of its children under the pricing measure p = 1/2, independent of the true chance (Hull ch. 13 applies the same risk-neutral argument to the one-step binomial; the lesson's own transfer screen draws that link). `puzzles-L4-concepts-r2.py`: "the game-by-game bets replicate +-100 exactly for a true game chance of 0.3, 0.5, 0.8" (300 paths each) and "binomial formula equals the backward-induction bet in every state". | Replace the hint with: "At evens a node is worth the average of its two children, whatever the true chance of a game: the bets copy the payoff, they do not predict it."

## Level 5

2. l26-estimating-n.json | `.screens[3].feedback.correct` | imprecise | "74: fair on average and the least noisy." Read across the three candidates on the screen, "the least noisy" is false: the raw maximum (option b, 60) has the smallest variance of the three, Var(m) = (k/(k+1))² Var(m + m/k − 1) = 0.64 Var(74-estimate) at k = 4; it is biased, which is the real objection. The max-based estimate is the least noisy of the unbiased estimates (it is the UMVUE), which is what the hint and the `why` for option c say. A learner who recalls "least noisy" without "among the fair ones" has the bias-variance comparison backwards. | Var(m) = k(N+1)(N−k)/((k+1)²(k+2)) and Var(2X̄ − 1) = (N+1)(N−k)/(3k) (sampling without replacement), so Var(mean-based)/Var(max-based) = (k+2)/3 = 2 at k = 4 and Var(m)/Var(max-based) = (k/(k+1))². Casella and Berger ch. 7 (Lehmann–Scheffé; the uniform-endpoint UMVUE), Goodman (1954) for the discrete case. `puzzles-L5-concepts-r2.py`: EXPECTED_FAIL "the max-based estimate is not less noisy than the raw maximum (Var ratio 1.562)", exhaustive over every sample of 4 from 1 to 30; the corrected assert sits beside it. | Replace with: "74: fair on average, and the least noisy of the fair estimates. The bare 60 varies less but sits below $N$ on average."

3. l27-given-the-total.json | `.summary.ideas[2]` | imprecise | "After conditioning on a total or a maximum, the dice are no longer independent or uniform." Given a total the first die is uniform on its feasible range; for a total of 7 it is uniform on 1 to 6, exactly as before, and for a total of $n + 1$ on $n$-sided dice (screens[10]) each die is uniform on 1 to $n$. Only the maximum makes the other die non-uniform (the trap screen's point: 5 with chance 1/9, each of 1 to 4 with 2/9). The summary turns a fact about the maximum into a rule for both conditions, and the "given a total" half is false as stated. Dependence does fail in both cases. | Enumeration of the 36 rolls: P(first die = a | total 7) = 1/6 for every a; P(first = a, second = b | total 7) ≠ P(first = a | total 7) P(second = b | total 7). `puzzles-L5-concepts-r2.py`: EXPECTED_FAIL "given total 7 each die is still uniform on 1..6 ({1: '1/6', ..., 6: '1/6'})", plus "the dice are dependent given a total or a maximum" and "given max 5 the other die is 5 with 1/9 and each of 1..4 with 2/9: not uniform". Grimmett and Stirzaker 1.4 for the definition. | Replace with: "After conditioning on a total or a maximum the dice are no longer independent; given the maximum, the other die is not even uniform."
