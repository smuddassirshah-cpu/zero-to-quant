## Checked and clean

The paper's design is stated correctly throughout: 12-month formation and 6-month trading with monthly starts and six overlapping portfolios (GGR §2.1 to §2.2), cumulative total-return index normalised to 1 (§2.1), exclusion of shares with a non-trading day (§2.1), exhaustive SSD matching with top 5, top 20 and pairs 101 to 120 (§2.1), the 2 historical standard deviation trigger from the formation period (§2.2), closing at the next crossing or the end of the trading interval with reopening allowed (§2.2), the delisting rule (§2.2), $1 long and $1 short (§2.4), committed against employed capital (§2.4), daily marking to market with monthly compounding (§2.4), payoffs as excess returns on a self-financing position (§2.4), monthly reporting (Table 1), the one-day wait against bid-ask bounce (§2.2, Table 1 Panel B), "up to 11%" annualised (abstract), survival of conservative cost estimates (abstract, §4.3), lower profits in later years (§5), factor regressions leaving the alpha (§4.1), the common latent factor (§5), no stop-loss. The course quotes only those paper facts and never a Table 1 cell, so no paper number is misquoted. Look-ahead: every `trade_pair`, `blotter`, `trade_coint` and `sweep` solution estimates the sd, hedge ratio and settings on formation days or in-sample cycles only; `held_out` runs the pre-chosen setting once; no solution peeks at trading-period data (ledger rows "code"). Statistical claims that simulate as stated: employed ≥ committed when the total is positive and equal when all open; the bounce lifts same-day payoffs on closes and the wait removes it while costing a day of convergence; overlapping six-month cycles inflate a naive t (sd 2.6 under the null) while Newey-West(5) nearly restores it (1.3) and leaves the mean unbiased; a planted pair that reaches 4 sd usually still crosses, so the stop costs money on the panel; the band exit books less in more trips; Engle-Granger power on a planted pair over one year is about 70%, so 5 of 20 failing is typical; the 30 closest chance pairs by SSD pass Engle-Granger about 28% of the time (selection bias), far above 5%; a hedge ratio of 2 is found by Engle-Granger and ranked far down by distance; scaling a price leaves the Engle-Granger p-value unchanged; low formation p-values precede the planted break; pairs that stop co-moving converge far less often while faster reversion (crowding) keeps the crossing share intact; dollar-neutral pairs with equal market loadings have beta near 0 yet lose in some cycles; the best of 12 equal settings falls back out of sample (winner's curse). Conventions are stated where they first matter: population sd via `np.std` (l2 screens[3], review-1 items[3]), monthly = cycle / 6 (l4 screens[2], l8 screens[3]), compounding for annualising (l4 screens[4]), per-pair returns (l8 screens[5]), four trades per round trip (l9 screens[4]), convergence share per trade (l11 screens[8]), the 0 / 0 rule (l4 screens[6]), the survival line (l15 screens[8]). `wrong[]` values and distractor `why` lines in the three levels encode the named slips correctly, with the exceptions in findings 1, 2 and 6.

Unverified (not findings, recorded for the fixer): whether GGR continue the formation normalisation through the trading period or re-base at the trading start (finding 4 depends on it); l8 screens[3] "averages the six live portfolios" (GGR §2.4 as read: six overlapping portfolios whose returns are averaged).
