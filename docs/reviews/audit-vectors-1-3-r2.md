## What was checked

Every stated figure, key and slip value was recomputed during the read, and the main ones again in the script. Every choice item was read for a second true option. Every canvas region and slider range that carries a feedback line was checked over its whole extent, with the engine's matching rule read from `app/js/engine/widgets/drag-point.js`. The watch list held up as follows.

- Dot product, angle, Cauchy-Schwarz, triangle inequality, norms and projection: correct, with the non-zero and same-direction conditions stated where they matter (l3 screens 7 and 8, summary).
- Correlation as a cosine and beta as a projection coefficient: correct, and equal to `np.corrcoef` and the least-squares slope on 2,000 simulated samples.
- Composition and commuting: l7 screen 1's key (a shear and a turn commute only when $k = 0$ or the turn is 0° or 180°) follows from $\mathbf{R}\mathbf{S} - \mathbf{S}\mathbf{R} = k\sin\theta\,\mathrm{diag}(-1, 1)$. The half-turn sentence is the scalar-multiple-of-$\mathbf{I}$ case and is right.
- Inverses and singular matrices: the 2 by 2 rule, the reverse-order rule and "singular exactly when the determinant is 0" are right. No content line tests a computed determinant with `==`, and l12 and homework-3 teach `matrix_rank` against both an exact test and a cut-off.
- `solve` against `inv`: the console figures of l12 screen 5 (4.4e-16 and 5.2e-05) reproduce exactly under Pyodide. `solve` left the smaller residual in all 300 random ill-conditioned systems tried. "About three times the arithmetic" matches the operation count ($n^3$ against $n^3/3$ multiplications).
- Portfolio applications: returns, leverage, shorts, P&L, factor exposures, $\mathbf{w}^T\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{w}$ and minimum-variance weights are right. The variance formula agrees with 400,000 simulated draws, and the weights minimise variance against 50,000 random mixes per matrix.
