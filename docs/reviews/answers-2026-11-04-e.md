## Notes

1. `l7-data-races.json` s6 claims "the compiler may read `done` once and spin forever" at `-O1`. With g++ `-O1` the load is hoisted and the loop spins. With clang++ `-O1` (the app's C++ toolchain) the loop is deleted and `spin` is a bare `ret`: the forward-progress rule lets clang assume a loop with no side effects ends. The key stays right because the item says "may", but the explanation could say "or remove the loop altogether". The same lines are shown in the `distractor` "Nothing" and "slower", both still wrong.
2. `l6-threads.json` s8 (ratio 240) and `homework-2.json` h2-counter (ratio 480) ignore the merge adds (3 and 7 adds of 1 ns), so exact values are 239.997 and 479.99. `tol.abs` is 0 for both. A learner who includes the merge fails. The prose says "then combine four sums: 3 adds" in l6 s8; suggest `tol: {"rel": 0.001}` or a sentence saying the merge is ignored.
3. `l8-locks-and-deadlock.json` s7: re-locking a held `std::mutex` is undefined behaviour in the standard; deadlock is what glibc does. "A deadlock" is fine for the canvas, and no memory-order or lock-order claim depends on it.
4. `l9-atomics.json` s9 and `homework-2.json` h2-store-buffering part 3 say only `seq_cst` forbids (0, 0). Relaxed accesses with `seq_cst` fences between each store and load also forbid it. The question asks for the order on all four accesses, so the key is right; "Only" in the l9 s9 feedback slightly overreaches.
5. Hardware and C++ claims checked and correct: the store-buffering (0, 0) result is allowed by `relaxed` and forbidden by `seq_cst`; x86 also lets a later load pass an earlier buffered store; release and acquire do not forbid it (store-to-load reordering); `release` forbids moving earlier writes after the store and `acquire` forbids moving later reads before the load; `x++` is `add dword ptr [x], 1` at `-O2` and still races; `lock add` is the atomic form; a `seq_cst` store on x86 is an `xchg`; `std::scoped_lock` avoids deadlock whatever the argument order; a notify wakes only current waiters; spurious wakeups exist; `while` re-check after `wait`; `thread_local`, `static` local and heap lifetimes in l6.
6. `l6-threads.json` s7 feedback calls `hits` and `m` "statics". `m` is a file-scope global, which has static storage duration. Loose, not wrong.
7. `homework-2.json` h2-position part 3 `wrong` ids are `six` (value 600) and `two` (value 200). The ids only key the feedback and are not shown, so harmless, but they mislead an author reading the file.
