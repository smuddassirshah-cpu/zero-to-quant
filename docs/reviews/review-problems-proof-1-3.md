# Problem sets, proof levels 1 to 3: second-reader findings

Scope: `app/content/courses/proof/problems-1.json`, `-2`, `-3`: 11 problems, 46 parts (31 numeric, 5 `expression`, 10 choice), 76 `wrong` entries. Rules: `docs/briefs/chained.md`. Pattern: `review-problems-1.4-c.md`.

Method. The author's script was run first. Each problem was read whole from its text (`review-problems-proof-1-3-scripts/show.py`), then solved again in `mine.py`, which imports nothing from the author's script and types every figure from the prose. Route 1 is a formula (integers, sympy). Route 2 builds the objects and counts them: the 40 accounts, the 8 rows, a scan of every threshold, the 40 numbers, all 1,296 assignments, every function from a 4-set to a 4-set with every pair of subsets, integer boxes, sums term by term. Every `wrong` value and `wrong[].tex` was rebuilt from the slip its line names, and each number in a `feedback`, `byKey`, `why` or `explain` string was recomputed while reading. Claims were checked as claims: each stated implication, each "converse", "negation" and "contrapositive", each "true here" and each counterexample, by search over a box. Every typed part then went through the engine's own graders (`grade-probe.mjs`: `numGrade`, `exprGrade` with the local Compute Engine): the key, with one more place, after a letter and `=`, with a true minus sign; each `accept` form; each `wrong` entry on its own key; and 217 strings of my own (a count with its noun, other orders and factorings of each bracket, a slip typed in another form, the answer one step short). Freshness was read by eye: `taught.py` prints every lesson screen, quiz item, review item and homework item of the three levels and every item of `exams/topic-0.6.json` and the proof part of `exams/stage-0.json` (title, prose, question, key), and `peek.py` prints an item whole. `fresh.py` then counts each figure, formula and story noun across `app/content`. `proposed.py` computes the numbers of every fix and checks the nouns of the proposed re-settings are new to the course.

Tags: `wrong` = a learner is marked wrongly or told something false; `unclear` = the question can be read two ways, does not say the form wanted, or refuses a sensible entry; `minor` = weak part, loose chain, missing restatement, wording or freshness.
