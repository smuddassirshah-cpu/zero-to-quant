## Rules as taught (checked across lessons, review and homework)

Sound, with their conditions: $\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}$ for $a, b \ge 0$, proved for positive $a$, $b$ with the positivity line and the reason it matters (l14 s8); simplest form as "no square factor above 1 left inside"; conjugates through $(p - \sqrt{q})(p + \sqrt{q}) = p^2 - q$; $\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{a} = a$ only for $a \ge 0$ (quiz). $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$ does not arise. Cancelling as $\tfrac{AC}{BC} = \tfrac{A}{B}$ for $B, C \ne 0$, with the excluded values listed on every simplification, the hole at $(-2, -1)$ drawn, and the divisor's zero ($x = -1$) excluded in the division example. Rejected roots are checked in the original in l16 s7, the quiz, the review and the homework. The factor theorem is stated "exactly when ... in both directions" and derived from $f(x) = (x - a)q(x) + r$; repeated root and one-real-root cubics both shown. The circle's equation is derived both ways (l18 s2 prose, and s9 with "both sides are at least 0, so no point is gained or lost"); the point circle and the empty case are covered. The discriminant's three cases, taking $y$ from the line, the vertical line and the lost root on dividing by $x$ are all present. Exceptions are findings 1, 3 to 7 and 10 to 16.
