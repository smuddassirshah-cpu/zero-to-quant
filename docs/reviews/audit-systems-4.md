## Findings

1. l16-ring-buffer.json | screens[6].explain.text | wrong | "A race needs two writers of one variable." A data race needs one writer and any other unordered access, not two writers; a learner carries away that a single-writer plain variable read by another thread is safe, which is exactly the bug the ring's atomics exist to prevent. | N4659 [intro.races]/2 and /21 (conflicting actions: one modifies, the other accesses; a data race is two conflicting actions in different threads, at least one non-atomic, neither happening before the other). l7-data-races teaches it correctly ("at least one writes"). `systems-L4-concepts.py`: l16 screens[6] explain, ThreadSanitizer reports one writer plus one reader of a plain `int` (EXPECTED_FAIL). | Replace the explain with: "A data race is two threads touching one variable, at least one writing, with nothing ordering them. Here each counter is an atomic with one writer, and the release and acquire pair hands each slot across before its counter moves. That is why one producer and one consumer need no lock."

2. l16-ring-buffer.json | screens[8].explain.text | wrong | "Unsigned subtraction stays right across the wrap, because 2^32 is a multiple of N." The conclusion is right and the reason is false: unsigned subtraction is exact modulo 2^32 for any N (with N = 3, tail 1 and head 4,294,967,295 still give 2). What needs N to divide 2^32 is the slot mapping counter mod N, which screens[2] shows breaking for N = 3. The sentence conflates the two facts the lesson just separated. | N4659 [basic.fundamental]/2 (unsigned arithmetic is modulo 2^N). `systems-L4-concepts.py`: l16 screens[8] explain (EXPECTED_FAIL: the C++ difference is 2 for a ring of 3 while the slots 1 and 0 show the mask breaking). | Replace the third sentence with: "Unsigned subtraction is exact modulo 2^32, so tail minus head stays right across the wrap for any N. It is the slot, counter mod N, that needs N to divide 2^32, which is why N is a power of two."

3. l16-ring-buffer.json | screens[4].explain.text | misleading | "With `alignas(64)` each counter keeps its own line and the cost falls to 10 ms." Padding removes the false sharing but not the true sharing: the producer's store to `tail_` still invalidates the consumer's copy of that line, which the consumer re-reads on every pop (and the consumer's `head_` store does the same to the producer), so neither line "stays put" at the 1 ns a push the screen's model assumes. The learner carries away that padding alone makes the counters local. | Drepper §3.3.4 (a write to a line held by another core invalidates it) and §6.4.1; Williams §8.2.3; the standard remedy is a cached copy of the other side's counter, re-read only when the ring looks full or empty (Rigtorp `SPSCQueue`, LMAX Disruptor 2011 §3.3). No simulation: a wall-clock check is out of scope. | Replace the explain with: "Stealing the line costs 600 ms of every second, 60% of a core, for a correct queue. With `alignas(64)` a store to one counter no longer evicts the other, which in this model is 10 ms. Each counter's line still moves when the other side reads it, so real rings also keep a cached copy of the other counter and re-read it only when the ring looks full or empty."

4. l16-ring-buffer.json | screens[3].explain.text | imprecise | "Neither counter is shared, yet the line ping-pongs: false sharing." Both counters are shared in the lesson's own design: the producer loads `head_` (screens[5], line s2) and the consumer loads `tail_` (homework-4 items[4], line s2). What is false is the sharing of the line between the two writers, not the sharing of the counters. | Drepper §6.4.1; Williams §8.2.3; the lesson's own push and pop orders. | Replace the last sentence with: "Each counter has one writer, yet every store drags the other counter's word along with it: false sharing."

Driven by two threads this solution is a data race. A learner may carry the alignas-only version away as the SPSC queue. | N4659 [intro.races]/21. `systems-L4-concepts.py`: l16 screens[9] widget.solution, ThreadSanitizer reports the race on `tail_` and the slots when a second thread pushes (EXPECTED_FAIL). | Minimal fix, one sentence added to the prose after "a test compares their addresses.": "This version is driven by one thread, so its counters are plain `size_t`; the two-thread ring makes them `std::atomic` and uses the loads and stores of the push screen." 6. l17-measuring-latency.json | screens[9].prose, hints[0].text, feedback.byKey.r0c1, explain.text, and quiz[3].widget.options[1].why | imprecise | "Timing one 10 ns call alone adds two reads to it", so the call "reads as 50 ns" and the clock is 80% of it. 8. l15-no-allocation.json | screens[4].explain.text | imprecise | "The pool is 25 times cheaper, and its cost never varies." "Never varies" turns a bounded cost into a constant one. | Drepper §3.3; the lesson's own level 1 numbers. | Replace with: "The pool is 25 times cheaper, and it has no slow path: no system call can hide inside a pop."

## Checked and clean

Allocation claims: every line the lessons say allocates does, and every line they say does not, does not, counted by a replaced `operator new` at `-O1 -fno-builtin` with both `-std=c++17` and `-std=c++20` (first `push_back` or `emplace_back` on an empty vector, `new`, `std::string` of 64 or 100 characters, a `std::map` insert; not a vector declaration, `std::array`, a plain array, or pushes after `reserve` with `clear` between messages, which keeps the capacity by N4659 [vector.capacity]/6). The libstdc++ small-string limit is 15 characters (libc++ 22), so the 64 and 100 figures are safe on both. Pool semantics (a LIFO free list, −1 when full) match every hand-traced sequence. All six code items compile and pass every hidden test with the model solution and fail with the starter, at both standards; the allocation-counted ones (format_price, Pool, Handler, LatencyHist) report 0 after construction, and the starters of format_price and Handler allocate, as their explains say. The ring arithmetic (mask, size, wrap at 2^32, slot order for a power of two and the collision for a ring of 3, 429.5 s to wrap) is right. The push and pop orders (relaxed own counter, acquire the other, release after the slot access) are the standard SPSC protocol (Williams §7.2) and deliver 200,000 items once each in order on two threads with ThreadSanitizer clean; each distractor's why is right. The `alignas(64)` solution puts the counters 64 bytes apart. The nearest-rank convention is stated once where it first matters (l17 screens[1]) and used consistently in every key, including the review and homework; the float boundary claims (999.0000000000001, 161.00000000000003) hold in Python and C++ doubles and NumPy's `inverted_cdf` reproduces the rank-1,000 error. Coordinated omission: the two reports, the 875 late sends and the true mean are recomputed from `latencies.csv`; the sent/intended difference is exactly the wait. Fleet percentiles pool, not average. The feed-handler policy (drop below, gap above, partial frames wait) gives the stated applied set and gap count; `sizeof(Handler)` is 18,032 as screens[3] states and the frame layout matches l14 (sequence big-endian at offsets 4 to 7). LatencyHist buckets, the clamp at 39 and the 2^(b+1) bound are right. The budget, sizing and residency arithmetic is right throughout.
