## Level 1

1. l4-gbm.json | quiz[2].explain.text | wrong | The explain ends "For small steps all three agree to first order." The additive update $S \leftarrow S + \mu\Delta t + \sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z$ is not a small-step approximation of GBM: it moves the price by pounds, so its noise is $\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z$ against the GBM step's $S\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z$. At $S = 100$ they disagree at order $\sqrt{\Delta t}$, not $\Delta t$. | `ito-L1-concepts.py:additive_update_not_first_order` (EXPECTED_FAIL): at $\Delta t = 1/252$ the mean gap between the additive and the exact step is 0.997, against 0.016 for Euler, which is of order $\sigma^2\Delta t$ as the lesson says on screen 7. Euler-Maruyama is the first-order scheme for GBM (Kloeden and Platen, *Numerical Solution of SDEs*, 10.2); the additive scheme discretises arithmetic Brownian motion, a different model. | Replace the explain with: "The exact log step multiplies by an exponential, which is always positive. The other two can produce negative prices when $Z$ is very negative. For small steps the Euler update agrees with the exact one to first order; the additive update is a different model, a price that moves by pounds rather than by a proportion."

2. l2-normal-increments.json | screens[7].explain.text | imprecise | "It grows without bound as $\Delta t \to 0$, so no slope exists anywhere. Brownian paths are continuous everywhere and differentiable nowhere." The sd argument shows that at any fixed time the difference quotient diverges, so the path has no derivative at that time (almost surely, for each fixed $t$). That almost every path has no derivative at any time at all is a separate and harder theorem; the "so" presents it as a consequence of the table. | Paley, Wiener and Zygmund (1933); Karatzas and Shreve, *Brownian Motion and Stochastic Calculus*, Theorem 2.9.18; Shreve, *Stochastic Calculus for Finance II*, remark after Theorem 3.4.3. The sd of the quotient is $1/\sqrt{\Delta t}$ (ledger row, `ito-L1-concepts.py`), which is the fixed-time statement. | Replace the last two sentences with: "It grows without bound as $\Delta t \to 0$, so at any fixed time the path has no slope. A stronger theorem (Paley, Wiener and Zygmund; Karatzas and Shreve, Theorem 2.9.18) says almost every path is continuous everywhere and differentiable nowhere."

## Level 2

3. l5-integrating-noise.json | screens[7].prose; summary.ideas[2] | imprecise | The isometry and "Itô integrals have mean 0" are stated with no hypothesis on the integrand. Both need $H$ chosen without look-ahead (adapted) and $E[\int_0^T H^2\,dt] < \infty$; without the second, the integral is only a local martingale and may have no second moment or a non-zero mean. The lesson builds adaptedness carefully (screens 0, 5, 10) but never states the integrability, and the summary is what a learner carries to an interview. Homework-2 items[8] states the condition correctly, so the fix only aligns the lesson with its own homework. | Shreve, *Stochastic Calculus for Finance II*, Theorem 4.3.1 (martingale property and isometry for $E\int\Delta^2\,du < \infty$); Karatzas and Shreve 3.2 (local martingales). | screens[7].prose: "The Itô isometry says $E\big[\big(\int_0^T H\,dW\big)^2\big] = E\big[\int_0^T H^2\,dt\big]$ for a position $H$ chosen without look-ahead and with $E\big[\int_0^T H^2\,dt\big] < \infty$. Take $H = W$ on $[0, 1]$, where $E[W_t^2] = t$." summary.ideas[2]: "For a position chosen without look-ahead and with $E[\int H^2\,dt] < \infty$, the Itô integral has mean 0 and satisfies the isometry $E[(\int H\,dW)^2] = E[\int H^2\,dt]$; a deterministic $f$ gives a normal with variance $\int f^2\,dt$."

4. l8-fair-games.json | screens[1].explain.text; screens[2].prose; summary.ideas[0]; summary.formula.note (and the recap in review-1.json items[4].prose) | imprecise | The lesson's rule is "$a = 0 \Rightarrow X$ is a martingale" (summary formula), "a process is a martingale when its $dt$ coefficient is zero at every time" (screen 1) and "A zero means a martingale" (screen 2). Zero drift gives a local martingale; a true martingale needs integrability, for example $E[\int_0^T b^2\,dt] < \infty$. The definition on summary.ideas[0] and review-1 items[4] also omits $E|X_t| < \infty$ and adaptedness. The lesson does say on screen 8 that "a zero-drift process can also fail to be a true martingale", which is right, but the formula panel and the summary are stated as unconditional rules, and the brief lists exactly this hypothesis as one a learner drops. Every example in the lesson does satisfy the condition, so the keys are unaffected. | Shreve Definition 2.3.5 (adapted, integrable), Theorem 4.3.1 (the Itô integral is a martingale when $E\int\Delta^2 < \infty$), Karatzas and Shreve 3.3 (local martingales; the classical counterexample is $1/\lvert B_t\rvert$ for three-dimensional $B$ started away from 0, a zero-drift process whose mean falls). Checked by `ito-L2-concepts.py:hermite_martingales` and `exponential_martingale` that every candidate in the lesson, review and homework is square-integrable, so the stated verdicts stand. | summary.formula.note: "Here $dX = a\,dt + b\,dW$. Compute $a$ with Itô's lemma, including the $\tfrac12 f_{xx}$ term. The rule needs $E[\int_0^T b^2\,dt] < \infty$; without it a zero-drift process is only a local martingale (screen 9)." screens[1].explain.text, last sentence: "In Itô form, a process is a martingale when its $dt$ coefficient is zero at every time and its noise term is square-integrable, $E[\int b^2\,dt] < \infty$; every process in this lesson passes that test." screens[2].prose: replace "A zero means a martingale." with "A zero means a martingale (all four have square-integrable noise)." summary.ideas[0]: "A martingale is a fair game: an adapted process with $E|X_t| < \infty$ and $E[X_t \mid \text{past to } s] = X_s$. In Itô form, its $dt$ coefficient is zero at every time." review-1.json items[4].prose: "From markov: $X_t$ is a martingale if $E|X_t| < \infty$ and $E[X_t \mid \text{the path up to } s] = X_s$ for every $s < t$. ..."

## Level 3

5. l11-prices-as-expectations.json | screens[3].prose; summary.ideas[1] | imprecise | "the same draws, half the noise" and "Antithetic pairs halve it on the same draws" state the lesson's one example as a rule. The halving holds for this at-the-money call (0.0465 to 0.0232) and not in general: the course's own homework-3 items[4] gets 0.0617 to 0.0372 out of the money, a ratio of 1.66, and says so. A learner who carries "antithetic halves the error" will mis-size a simulation. | Glasserman, *Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering*, 4.2: the variance reduction is $\tfrac12(1 + \rho)$ with $\rho$ the correlation of the paired payoffs, which depends on the payoff. `ito-L3-concepts.py:antithetic_not_always_half` (EXPECTED_FAIL): the ratio is 2.00 at the money and 1.66 out of the money on the course's own seeds. | screens[3].prose, last sentence: "With `antithetic=True`, average the payoffs for $z$ and $-z$ before discounting: the same draws, and here half the noise." summary.ideas[1]: "A simulation's standard error is $s/\sqrt n$: four times the draws halve it. Report the price with it. Antithetic pairs cut it on the same draws, by half for this call and by less out of the money."

6. l12-barrier-option.json | screens[3].explain.text | imprecise | "More than half the paths touch £90 in a year here, and those paths are the lost value." True under the pricing measure (the probability that GBM with drift $r - \sigma^2/2 = 0.03$ falls to 90 within a year is 0.551) and false under the real drift of 12% (0.441). The sentence names no measure, two screens after lesson 10 taught that $Q$ forecasts nothing, so a learner reads it as a statement about real paths. | First-passage law for Brownian motion with drift (Shreve, *Stochastic Calculus for Finance II*, Corollary 7.2.2; Hull, *Options, Futures and Other Derivatives*, 26.9): $P(\min S \le B) = \Phi\big(\frac{b - \nu T}{\sigma\sqrt T}\big) + e^{2\nu b/\sigma^2}\Phi\big(\frac{b + \nu T}{\sigma\sqrt T}\big)$ with $b = \ln(B/S_0)$. `ito-L3-concepts.py:barrier_touch_measure`: 0.551 under $Q$, 0.441 under $P$, closed form and bridge-corrected simulation agree. | Replace the sentence with: "Under the pricing measure more than half the paths (55%) touch £90 in a year, and those paths are the lost value; with the real drift of 12% it is 44%, but a price uses $Q$."

7. homework-3.json | items[6].model, section 5 (last sentence) | misleading | "Continuous hedging and no jumps also fail, so a barrier option's delta can jump near the barrier." The delta of a knock-out is large and discontinuous at the barrier inside the Black-Scholes model itself: the value drops to zero on a touch. The failure of continuous hedging and the presence of jumps do not cause that; they mean the seller cannot rebalance a delta that is changing fast, and carries gap risk. The sentence gives the wrong cause for a true effect, in a model answer the learner marks themselves against. | Hull 26.9 (barrier options: "delta can be discontinuous at the barrier"); Taleb, *Dynamic Hedging*, chapter on barrier options; the knock-out formula on l12 screens[3] has $c_{do} \to 0$ as $S \downarrow B$ with $c_{do} > 0$ for $S > B$. | Replace with: "Continuous hedging and no jumps also fail: a barrier option's delta is already large and discontinuous at the barrier inside the model, so with discrete hedging or a jump the seller cannot rebalance in time and carries gap risk."
