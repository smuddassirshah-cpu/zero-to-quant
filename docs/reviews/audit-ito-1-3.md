1. l4-gbm.json | quiz[2].explain.text | wrong | The explain ends "For small steps all three agree to first order." The additive update $S \leftarrow S + \mu\Delta t + \sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z$ is not a small-step approximation of GBM: it moves the price by pounds, so its noise is $\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z$ against the GBM step's $S\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z$. At $S = 100$ they disagree at order $\sqrt{\Delta t}$, not $\Delta t$. | `ito-L1-concepts.py:additive_update_not_first_order` (EXPECTED_FAIL): at $\Delta t = 1/252$ the mean gap between the additive and the exact step is 0.997, against 0.016 for Euler, which is of order $\sigma^2\Delta t$ as the lesson says on screen 7. The other two can produce negative prices when $Z$ is very negative. 2. l2-normal-increments.json | screens[7].explain.text | imprecise | "It grows without bound as $\Delta t \to 0$, so no slope exists anywhere. That almost every path has no derivative at any time at all is a separate and harder theorem; the "so" presents it as a consequence of the table. | Paley, Wiener and Zygmund (1933); Karatzas and Shreve, *Brownian Motion and Stochastic Calculus*, Theorem 2.9.18; Shreve, *Stochastic Calculus for Finance II*, remark after Theorem 3.4.3. ## Level 3

5. l11-prices-as-expectations.json | screens[3].prose; summary.ideas[1] | imprecise | "the same draws, half the noise" and "Antithetic pairs halve it on the same draws" state the lesson's one example as a rule. A learner who carries "antithetic halves the error" will mis-size a simulation. | Glasserman, *Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering*, 4.2: the variance reduction is $\tfrac12(1 + \rho)$ with $\rho$ the correlation of the paired payoffs, which depends on the payoff. `ito-L3-concepts.py:antithetic_not_always_half` (EXPECTED_FAIL): the ratio is 2.00 at the money and 1.66 out of the money on the course's own seeds. | screens[3].prose, last sentence: "With `antithetic=True`, average the payoffs for $z$ and $-z$ before discounting: the same draws, and here half the noise." summary.ideas[1]: "A simulation's standard error is $s/\sqrt n$: four times the draws halve it. 6. l12-barrier-option.json | screens[3].explain.text | imprecise | "More than half the paths touch £90 in a year here, and those paths are the lost value." True under the pricing measure (the probability that GBM with drift $r - \sigma^2/2 = 0.03$ falls to 90 within a year is 0.551) and false under the real drift of 12% (0.441). The sentence names no measure, two screens after lesson 10 taught that $Q$ forecasts nothing, so a learner reads it as a statement about real paths. | First-passage law for Brownian motion with drift (Shreve, *Stochastic Calculus for Finance II*, Corollary 7.2.2; Hull, *Options, Futures and Other Derivatives*, 26.9): $P(\min S \le B) = \Phi\big(\frac{b - \nu T}{\sigma\sqrt T}\big) + e^{2\nu b/\sigma^2}\Phi\big(\frac{b + \nu T}{\sigma\sqrt T}\big)$ with $b = \ln(B/S_0)$. `ito-L3-concepts.py:barrier_touch_measure`: 0.551 under $Q$, 0.441 under $P$, closed form and bridge-corrected simulation agree. | Replace the sentence with: "Under the pricing measure more than half the paths (55%) touch £90 in a year, and those paths are the lost value; with the real drift of 12% it is 44%, but a price uses $Q$."

7. homework-3.json | items[6].model, section 5 (last sentence) | misleading | "Continuous hedging and no jumps also fail, so a barrier option's delta can jump near the barrier." The delta of a knock-out is large and discontinuous at the barrier inside the Black-Scholes model itself: the value drops to zero on a touch. The failure of continuous hedging and the presence of jumps do not cause that; they mean the seller cannot rebalance a delta that is changing fast, and carries gap risk. The sentence gives the wrong cause for a true effect, in a model answer the learner marks themselves against. | Hull 26.9 (barrier options: "delta can be discontinuous at the barrier"); Taleb, *Dynamic Hedging*, chapter on barrier options; the knock-out formula on l12 screens[3] has $c_{do} \to 0$ as $S \downarrow B$ with $c_{do} > 0$ for $S > B$. | Replace with: "Continuous hedging and no jumps also fail: a barrier option's delta is already large and discontinuous at the barrier inside the model, so with discrete hedging or a jump the seller cannot rebalance in time and carries gap risk."
