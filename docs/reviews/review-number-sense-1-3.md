## Continuity

Inside the course, notation is steady across L1 to L3. Fractions are written $\tfrac{a}{b}$ with "top" and "bottom" (finding 23 adds the GCSE names once). Rounding is half up everywhere ("5 or more rounds up": l6, l7, l13, review-2, homework-2), matching GCSE. Recurring decimals use the UK dot ($0.\dot{3}$). Order of operations is BIDMAS with × and ÷, then + and −, as tied ranks worked left to right, and $-3^2 = -9$ (l12, review-3), matching GCSE. Standard form is $a \times 10^n$ with $1 \le a < 10$. A billion is $10^9$ and a trillion $10^{12}$ (UK short scale). Significant figures ignore leading zeros (l13). Money is £ throughout.

Every in-course cross-reference was checked: l14 `quiz[3]` "as in the estimation lesson" (l13 `screens[4]`), l13 dividing by 0.5 against l5, l12 powers against l10, and l11 negatives squared against l9. All hold. The prose of l7 `screens[3]` names "powers of ten" three lessons before l10 defines a power, but as a name for 10, 100 and 1000 only; no change.

homework-3 `items[5]` (pin down $\sqrt{10}$ to two places) has the same shape as l11 `screens[8]`. It extends to hundredths and adds the midpoint check, so it is kept as a deliberate step up; finding 7 makes l11 teach that check first.

Downstream `requires` "why" lines, checked against the content: equations (L2: sign rules for brackets, l9 `screens[9]`), exp-log (L3: index laws and negative indices, l10 and l14), mental (L2: fractions to decimals by division, l7), ratio (L2: decimal conversion, l7), series (L3: powers, l10). All hold. quadratics and trig cite surds that are never taught: finding 30.

## Engine

1. The `numeric` widget compares values only, so it cannot require a stated number of decimal places (4.0 against 4, £13.00 against 13). GCSE marking needs the trailing zero for "give your answer to 1 decimal place". Finding 13 works around it in the text. A widget option such as `"places": 1` (accept only entries with exactly that many decimals, with a slip line otherwise) would let rounding items grade the convention.
2. The validator could warn when a `numeric` widget's `placeholder` parses to a value inside the answer or a `wrong[]` band. l3 `quiz[3]` shows "For example 3/7", the item's own add-tops-and-bottoms slip (finding 9).
3. The validator could warn on authoring jargon in learner-facing text ("edge case", "distractor", "misconception", "trap screen"). There were four "edge case" hits in number-sense alone (finding 15).

These three are appended to `docs/BACKLOG-INBOX.md` under "Raised later (review number-sense L1 to L3):".
