# Concept audit, round 3: numerics, levels 1 to 3

Third blind concept audit (brief: `docs/briefs/concept-audit.md`) of `app/content/courses/numerics/` levels 1 to 3: l1 to l12, review-1 to review-3, homework-1 to homework-3. Every screen, quiz item, hint, explain, why, feedback, rubric, model answer and wrong-option line was read, and every code widget's solution was run in CPython 3.13 (NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1): 127 hidden tests, 127 pass, and every starter fails. Claim ledger: `docs/reports/audit-numerics-1-3-r3-ledger.md` (188 rows: correct 179, imprecise 7, misleading 2, wrong 0, unverified 0). Experiments: `docs/reports/numerics-L1-concepts-r3.py` (80 checks), `-L2-concepts-r3.py` (73), `-L3-concepts-r3.py` (72).

Findings are `N. file | JSON path | severity | problem | evidence and basis | exact fix`. Severities: `wrong` (false as stated, or code that misbehaves against what the lesson claims), `imprecise` (true under an unstated hypothesis), `misleading` (true, but teaches a wrong habit). Each is tagged `serious` or `minor`.

**Result: 8 findings: 0 `wrong`, 7 `imprecise`, 1 `misleading`. All 8 are minor; none is serious.** No definition, theorem, convergence order, error formula, graded key or printed value in the three levels is false. Every fix below was applied to an in-memory copy of the content: each old string occurs exactly once, the changed solutions still pass all their hidden tests, and each new sentence is under 35 words.

## Checked and clean

Floating point: the 64-bit layout, bias 1023, hidden bit, gap $2^{k-52}$, $\epsilon = 2^{-52}$ as the gap above 1 and $u = 2^{-53}$ as the rounding bound (the two are kept apart everywhere, in the bump formulas too), subnormals to $2^{-1074}$, overflow in `math` and NumPy, float32 gaps, and Sterbenz's lemma (200,000 pairs in exact arithmetic). The rounding model was checked on 100,000 random decimals: worst error 0.996 $u$.

Conditioning and stability: $\kappa$ for $\sqrt x$, $e^x$, $x - 1$, $\ln x$, $x^2$, $e^x - 1$, the 2 by 2 system (40,002, and 28,285 for the one direction tried), the Hilbert matrix, the correlation matrix, and the sample variance ($2\bar x/s$). Residual against error. Backward stability and the forward bound. $\kappa(\mathbf A^T\mathbf A) = \kappa(\mathbf A)^2$ and $\kappa(\mathbf R) = \kappa(\mathbf A)$ on 200 random matrices. The Läuchli and Trefethen-Bau examples reproduce, with $-0.16$ on a desktop as the lesson says.

Root finding: orders 1, 1.618 and 2 with their hypotheses (simple root, $f' \ne 0$; order 1 at a double root is stated), the Newton constant, the efficiency indices and the 0.44 break-even. `safe_newton` survived 3,000 random brackets with rising, falling, flat and steep $f$ (no failure, at most 25 calls), roots at either end, same-sign values near $10^{-200}$, and the ninefold root with and without the halving rule. The residual-against-error wording on `ftol` is right. `implied_vols` matched the truth on 400 random options and returned NaN below the bounds.

Interpolation and splines: uniqueness, the error formula, Runge's numbers on both node sets (also in exact rationals), the node products, the Lebesgue constants 29.9 and 2.42, and the alternating-error swing. The spline system, its second derivatives against `CubicSpline`, forwards, bump responses and prices. The "never shows Runge's swings" line holds: the natural spline's error on Runge's function falls from 2.2e-2 to 1.6e-5 as the knots go from 11 to 81.

Level 3: both error formulas hold with their signs ($f''(\xi)$ and $f^{(4)}(\xi)$ recovered inside $[1, e]$). Richardson of the trapezium rule equals Simpson on 200 random integrands. With a kink inside a panel, error times $n^2$ swings between $-320$ and $107$ for Simpson and between $-160$ and $80$ for the trapezium rule: second order and erratic, as said. A kink on an even-numbered node restores ratios of 16; on an odd-numbered node it does not. Gauss rules for $n = 1$ to 8 are exact to degree $2n - 1$ and not $2n$. The CIR closed form agrees with DOP853 to 1e-11; local errors scale as $h^2$ and $h^5$; the two-run estimate has the right sign and size; an independent copy of the controller gives 38 accepted steps, 1 rejected, and $2.4 \times 10^{-8}$. The four stability functions, the drawn RK4 curve, the limit $-2.7853$, the paths at $h = 0.25$, and `solve_ivp`'s counts (1,172, 422, 226, the same in SciPy 1.18.1) all reproduce.
