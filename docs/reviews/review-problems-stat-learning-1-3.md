Method. The author's script was run first. Every part was then solved by hand from its text and again in `docs/reports/problems-stat-learning-numbers-read.py`, which imports nothing from the author's script and types every given from the prose. Route 1 is exact (fractions). Route 2 is another method: a minimiser for the best constant, `np.polyfit` on 50 random sets of distinct temperatures for the least interpolating degree, scikit-learn `KNeighborsRegressor` on raw and scaled features, a simulated mean of five noises, the $1/n$ rule as a function of $n$ with the crossing of the two degrees found by `brentq`, centred data built with $\sum xy = 90$ and $\sum x^2 = 100$ and fitted by `Ridge` in three units, an orthogonal design with $\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X} = n\mathbf{I}$ fitted by `Lasso` and `Ridge`, two million simulated test rows for each expected test MSE, a scan of expected costs for both thresholds, label arrays scored by scikit-learn, a row-level ECE, a position path for the flips. Every `wrong` value was rebuilt from the slip its line names, and every number in a feedback, `byKey`, `why` or `explain` string was matched to a given or a recomputed value (0 left over). Bands: the key band takes the exact value, the stated rounding (half up and half even), one more place, and the route its own feedback prints; no `wrong` band meets the key band or another `wrong` band. Claims were read against the lessons: the test $R^2$ of l5, the ridge factor $n/(n + \lambda)$ of l7, `Lasso(alpha)` as soft thresholding of l8, `ddof=0` scaling of l9, the cost threshold of l11, the ECE of l13, the $t$ against the base rate of l14.
