7. l11-feynman-kac.json | screens[2].prose and the ordered lines f3 to f5 (explain.text) | imprecise | The screen claims "$V(t, x) = E[e^{-r(T-t)}h(X_T) \mid X_t = x]$ solves $V_t + \mu V_x + \tfrac12\sigma^2V_{xx} - rV = 0$" and then proves the converse: if $V$ solves the equation, $M = e^{-rs}V(s, X_s)$ has no drift, is a martingale, and so $V$ equals the expectation (a verification theorem, which also needs the local martingale to be a true martingale). That the expectation solves the equation is the other direction: $M$ built from the expectation is a martingale by the tower property, Itô gives its $ds$ term, and a martingale's $ds$ term must vanish. The learner is told one statement and shown the proof of another. | Shreve II 6.4, proof of Theorem 6.4.1 and Theorem 6.4.3 (tower property, then Itô); the verification direction is Shreve II 6.4 Remark after Theorem 6.4.1 | screens[2].prose, first two sentences: "Now a general process $dX = \mu(X)\,dt + \sigma(X)\,dW$ and a rate $r$. We show that a solution of $V_t + \mu V_x + \tfrac12\sigma^2V_{xx} - rV = 0$ with $V(T, x) = h(x)$ must equal $E[e^{-r(T-t)}h(X_T) \mid X_t = x]$, so the expectation is the solution." explain.text: "The drift of $M$ is the left side of the equation, times $e^{-rs}$. Setting it to 0 makes $M$ a martingale, so its mean at $T$ equals its value now, and discounting is the $-rV$ term. Read the other way, the expectation makes $M$ a martingale by the tower property, so its $ds$ term must vanish: it solves the equation."
