## Claims checked by derivation

- `p-cubic-bracket`. $f' = 3x^2 + 6$ rises on $[0, 2]$, so $\sup |f'| = 18$ and the mean value theorem gives $|f(x) - f(y)| \le 18|x - y|$ on $[0, 2]$: one $\delta = \tfrac{0.009}{18}$ for every point (uniform continuity in all but name; the name is l20's and is rightly not used). Mean value hypotheses hold (polynomial). The intermediate value theorem is used with continuity and $f(0) < 0 < f(2)$ stated. The midpoint after 12 exact halvings lies within $\tfrac{1}{4096}$ of the root 1.5523 and $|f(m)| \le \tfrac{9}{2048}$ holds.
- `p-quartic-roots`. Extreme value theorem used on a closed bounded interval with continuity stated; candidates are end points and zeros of $h'$. Rolle: four roots in $[-1, 3]$ force three zeros of $h'$ strictly between the outer roots, so inside $(-1, 3)$; $h'$ has two there. Three sign changes give exactly 3 roots ($-0.628$, 0.628, 2.758).
- `p-bump-series`. "Converges uniformly on $[0, \infty)$": $0 \le f_k \le M_k = \tfrac{1}{ek^2}$ with the supremum attained at $\tfrac{1}{k^2}$, $\sum M_k$ converges, so the M-test applies and $\sup_x |S - S_N| \le \sum_{k > N} M_k < \tfrac{1}{eN}$ (integral comparison). True, and consistent with $S(x) \approx \tfrac12\sqrt{\pi x}$ near 0, which is continuous at 0. The uniform limit theorem is used with each $S_N$ continuous. The $\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}$ run at 0 is sound: $S(0) = S_{37}(0) = 0$, $S_{37}(x) \le 37x$.
- `p-root-sums`. Monotone, so suprema at right ends; gap telescopes to $\tfrac8n \to 0$, so integrable by l20's criterion. Part two of the fundamental theorem as l21 states it ($G' = f$ on all of $[a, b]$, $f$ integrable): $G = \tfrac23 x^{3/2}$ has the one-sided $G'(0) = 0 = \sqrt0$, and the explain says so.
- `p-diamond-ball`. "Open" in $d_1$, $d_2$, $d_\infty$: $B_1(y, r) \subseteq U$ for $r = 1 - d_1(0, y)$ by the triangle inequality; $d_1 \le \sqrt2\,d_2$ (Cauchy-Schwarz) and $d_1 \le 2\,d_\infty$ give the disc and the square. True. $\tfrac{0.3}{\sqrt2}$ is also the true distance from $p$ to the edge, so "largest" is safe on either reading. "Not closed": $(1 - \tfrac1n, 0) \to (1, 0) \notin U$. True.
- `p-accumulated-cubic`. Part one needs $f$ continuous (stated), part two $G' = f$ with $f$ integrable (holds). "Compact" for $[0, 5]$: closed and bounded in $\mathbb{R}$ (l24 Heine-Borel). "$[0, 5)$ is not closed, so not compact": $5 - \tfrac1n \in [0, 5)$ for $n \ge 1$ and tends to 5; sequence test of l23. True. Supremum $\tfrac{20}{3}$ on $[0, 5)$ is not attained ($F \le \tfrac43$ on $[0, 3]$, strictly rising on $(3, 5)$). $|f| \le 8$ on $[0, 5]$, attained at 5.
