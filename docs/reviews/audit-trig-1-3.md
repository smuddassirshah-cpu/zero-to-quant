## Checked and clean

Watch list from the brief, each read on every screen and item:

- Degrees against radians. Every angle carries its unit; `l7` defines the radian by arc length; graphs and models from `l8` on label the axis in radians; `l11` solves in degrees and gives one radian item; the modelling items say "the angle in radians". The only gap is the calculator mode in level 1 (finding 5). Every `degrees`, `radians`, `degree-mode` and `radian-mode` slip value was recomputed (script A: for example $10{,}000\cos(135\text{ rad}) = -9{,}960.88$, $200\sin(-3.67^\circ) + 500 = 487.21$).
- SOH CAH TOA only in right-angled triangles. `l2` defines the ratios in a right-angled triangle and names the sides from the angle; `problems-1` `items[2].parts[0]` rejects tangent "of a right-angled triangle only"; the three traps (`l1`, `l2`, `l3`) all turn on finding the right angle first.
- Sine and cosine rules. Both derivations are sound for the triangles drawn; the pairing trap (`l5` `screens[8]`) and its drawing are correct ($A = 30^\circ$, $C = 45^\circ$, $BC = 10$ by coordinates). Random-triangle check in script E. Findings 3 and 4 cover the ambiguous case and the obtuse case.
- Inverse functions' ranges and other solutions. `l11` gives the second solution by symmetry and the rest by period; the rules were checked against a root scan for 199 values of $k$ (sine, cosine) and 61 (tangent). `problems-3` `items[0].parts[4]` uses both signs to place $A + B$. Finding 6 covers the unstated ranges.
- Periodicity and all solutions in an interval. Every solution list and count was recomputed by root scan (script B), including the closed-interval ends in `review-3` `items[5]` (0° and 360° both count) and the tangency at 90° in `l11` `screens[8]`.
- Unit circle and signs by quadrant. Definitions on `l6` `screens[3]` and `screens[8]` are standard; the direction of measurement (anticlockwise from the positive x-axis, against a bearing) is stated.
- Identities with their domains. $\tan\theta = \tfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$ carries $(\cos\theta \ne 0)$ on `l10` `screens[7]`; the proof of the Pythagorean identity covers every quadrant and the axes; the compound-angle proof states $a > b$ and $a - b \le 180^\circ$ and handles the other case in its explanation.
- Small-angle approximations. None is taught. The one remark (`l3` `screens[7]`: sine and tangent answers are close because the angle is small) is true in either unit.
- Amplitude, period and phase. `l9` `screens[7]` states $|a|$, $\tfrac{2\pi}{b}$ with $b > 0$, shift $c$ to the right and midline $d$; the smallest period of every wave used was checked numerically; "phase shift" is used for $c$ (a time) and "phase" or "lag as an angle" for $bc$, consistently.
- atan against atan2. No item computes a direction from a single inverse tangent where the quadrant is in doubt. `homework-3` `items[8]` states "north of east" with both components positive.
- Fluency families over their whole range (script C): every key equals an independent formula, every stated 4 dp ratio is the correct rounding, no slip value ever equals the key or sits in another slip's band, every Pythagorean triple is exact, every radian fraction is in lowest terms.
- Canvas lines over their whole region (script D): every straight line on a static plane canvas ends on a drawn point; the bearings, the trap triangles and the sine-rule drawings were rebuilt from coordinates.
- The finance transfers. `l5` `screens[9]` (volatility as arrow length, correlation as the cosine, the spread as the third side) is correct and matches a 400,000-day simulation. `l12` `screens[9]` is a correct dot product. Findings 1 and 2 cover the two that are not.
