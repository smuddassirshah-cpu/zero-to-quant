# Topic 3.5 (coding-int): second-reader findings

Scope: every file in `app/content/courses/coding-int/` (l1 to l19, review-1 to review-4, homework-1 to homework-4; 27 content files plus `course.json`), the ten `ci-*` drills in `app/content/drills/`, and the link to `app/content/exams/mock-oa-code.json`.
Method: all 54 code widgets in lessons and homework (47 Python, 7 C++) and the 10 drills (5 Python, 5 C++) run in a harness against their own solution and starter (Python in CPython 3, C++ with `g++ -std=c++17 -O1 -fno-exceptions`). 1,130 single-edit mutants of the Python solutions and 40 per C++ solution were run against the hidden tests, then named wrong solutions were written for every survivor that is a plausible learner bug (`scratchpad/review-coding-int/mut.py`, `wrong.py`, `newtests.py`, `cmut.py`). Every proposed new test below was run: the model solution passes it and the wrong solution fails it. Prose read screen by screen for algorithmic claims. Layout checked with `playthrough.mjs --mobile --shots` on l1, l4, l9, l10 and l17 (all pass; screens at 390 px and desktop viewed for l1, l9, l10). Keys were not recomputed (`answers-2026-11-04-coding-int-3.md` and `-4.md` did that); I re-derived a few dozen of the numbers I met on the way and all agree.
Format: `path | screen/item | severity | what is wrong | fix`. Severity: error = a false claim, a gating break, or a plausible wrong solution that passes every hidden test; warning = weak test, weak item, teaching or metadata issue; note = style.

Counts: 6 errors, 16 warnings, 11 notes. Voice scan (`docs/reviews/voice-3.5-coding-int.txt`): course files 0 explanations over three sentences and 0 of 10,665 sentences over 20 words; the drill files have 14 sentences of 21 to 30 words (warning 20). No exclamation marks outside code, no em or en dashes, no American spellings, no dollar amounts (prices are in pence), no firm names outside the three in `mock-oa-code` `models` that DECISIONS "Firm names" allows. Mechanical checks: every choice and multi has `shuffle: true`, every distractor has a `why`, no numeric answer equals or sits within the tolerance of a `wrong` value, every `wrong` value has a `byKey` line, every code item and quiz item has `tags`; the keys are visibly longest in two items (warning 21) and borderline in nine (warning 21). All 54 model solutions pass their tests; every starter fails at least one test; fresh-numbers check (lists in review and homework items against lessons) finds two coincidental three-number lists only (note 32).
