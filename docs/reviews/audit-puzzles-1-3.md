### Level 1

1. l6-when-to-stop.json | screens[3].prose, screens[3].explain (and screens[7].lines, feedback.byKey.d1, which rest on it) | imprecise | "Hold at 20" is presented as the best rule for one-player pig, but the objective is never stated. The threshold 20 is optimal only for maximising the expected total of a single turn. Under the game's real objective (reach 100 first) the optimal policy is nowhere near a fixed threshold, and even a one-player race to a target in the fewest turns is not solved by 20. A learner carries away "pig: hold at 20" as a fact about the game. | Basis: the one-step look-ahead rule is optimal here because the problem is monotone (once a roll stops paying it never pays again, since $t$ only rises: Chow, Robbins and Siegmund ch. 3; Ferguson, *Optimal Stopping*, ch. 5 "the monotone case"); `puzzles-L1-concepts.py:check_pig_threshold` solves the one-turn DP exactly and finds 20, 9 and 15 for the three dice. For the real game see Neller and Presser, "Optimal play of the dice game Pig", *UMAP Journal* 25 (2004): the optimal policy depends on both scores and is far from hold-at-20. | Fix: screens[3].prose, replace the second sentence with "A 1 wipes $t$ and ends the turn, and your aim is the largest expected total from this one turn." Then screens[3].explain, replace the last sentence with "That is why one roll of look-ahead finds the best rule for this aim. In the race to 100 the best play is not a fixed threshold." (Two sentences replace one; the explain stays at three sentences if the first two are merged: "One more roll changes $t$ by $\tfrac{20 - t}{6}$ on average, positive below 20, and rolling only makes $t$ larger, so once a roll stops paying it never pays again.")

### Level 2

2. l9-bayes-in-a-minute.json | screens[8].widget.rows[0][0] (the drill prompt "Monty Hall: you pick a door, the host opens a goat door. Chance you win by switching?"), with feedback.byKey.r0c1 and explain.text | imprecise | The key $\tfrac23$ is right only under the standard protocol, which the prompt does not state: the host knows where the car is, always opens an unchosen door, and always shows a goat. Chance you win by switching?" and the explain's first sentence with "Switching wins whenever your first pick was wrong, $\tfrac23$, because the host always shows a goat behind another door."

### Level 3

3. l15-exceeding-one.json | screens[9].prose (and hints[0]) | imprecise | "Wald's identity says $E[S_N] = E[N]\,E[U]$ when $N$ depends only on the draws so far." The stopping-time property alone is not enough: Wald's equation also needs $E[N] < \infty$. The lesson's own neighbour supplies the counterexample: the symmetric walk stopped at $+1$ (l10 screens[2]) is a stopping time with $E[S_N] = 1$ while $E[\text{step}] = 0$, because $E[N] = \infty$. The conclusion $e/2$ is right here because $E[N] = e$, but the stated condition is the one the learner carries away. | Basis: Ross, *Introduction to Probability Models*, 7.3 Theorem 3.1 (Wald's equation: i.i.d. with finite mean, $N$ a stopping time with $E[N] < \infty$); Grimmett and Stirzaker 10.2; `puzzles-L3-concepts.py:check_wald` (simulated $E[S_N] = 1.359$ and the floored-walk limit showing $E[S_N] \to 1 \ne 0$ as $E[N] \to \infty$). | Fix: screens[9].prose, replace the last sentence with "Wald's identity says $E[S_N] = E[N]\,E[U]$ when $N$ is a stopping time with a finite mean, as here: $E[N] = e$." and hints[0] with "$N$ is a stopping time with finite mean: it depends only on draws so far, and $E[N] = e$."

### Level 2 (wrong)

Both conditional durations fall with the edge. From £3 to £10: $E[T \mid \text{win}]$ is 30.33 bets at $p = \tfrac12$ and 24.13 at $p = 0.6$; $E[T \mid \text{ruin}]$ is 17.00 at $p = \tfrac12$ and 12.36 at $p = 0.6$. The unconditional mean stays near 21 only because the mix shifts towards the long winning runs ($0.716 \times 24.1$ against $0.3 \times 30.3$). The sentence is offered as the reason 20.8 is close to 21, and that reason is false. | Basis: exact conditional durations by Doob's $h$-transform (the chain conditioned to hit 10 has up-step chance $p\,P_{k+1}/P_k$), solved as a linear system in `puzzles-L2-concepts.py:check_ruin_conditional`; the fair case matches the known $E[T \mid \text{win}] = (N^2 - k^2)/3$; an independent Monte Carlo of 400,000 runs gives 30.31 / 17.00 and 24.11 / 12.36. The script's check encodes the content's sentence and fails by design; its replacement assertion for the fix pass is in the script comment. | Fix: replace the third sentence with "The edge shortens both the winning runs and the losing ones, but far more runs are now the long winning kind." (The explain stays at three sentences.)
