# Concept audit: mm-games, levels 1 to 3

Blind concept audit (brief: `docs/briefs/concept-audit.md`) of `app/content/courses/mm-games/` levels 1 to 3: l1 to l13, review-1 to review-3, homework-1 to homework-3. Every screen, quiz item, hint, explain, why, feedback, rubric, model answer and wrong-option line was read, and every code widget's solution was run against its examples and hidden tests in CPython. SUMMARY_LINE

Findings follow the pattern `N. file | JSON path | severity, tag | problem | evidence and basis | exact fix`. Every fix is paste-ready text in the course voice. Paths use `screens[n]`, `quiz[n]`, `items[n]`.

## Level 1

1. `l1-unknown-quantity.json`, `homework-1.json` | `screens[4].explain.text`; `items[1].explain.text` | imprecise, minor | The lesson concludes from 7 hits in 10 that "the candidate was overconfident". The homework scorer returns `calibrated` for the same ten ranges, and review-1 says ten ranges are too few. The label `calibrated` is itself only a failure to reject. | Binomial(10, 0.9) gives P(hits ≤ 7) = 0.0702, above 0.05 (L1 script, exact and Monte Carlo). A non-rejection is not evidence for the null (Casella and Berger sec. 8.3). | l1 `screens[4].explain.text`: "Seven of ten ranges held the truth, a hit rate of 0.7 against the 0.9 promised. A calibrated forecaster scores 7 or fewer only about 7% of the time. That is a warning, not proof: the ranges look too narrow, which is overconfidence." homework-1 `items[1].explain.text`, first paragraph: "The p-value adds $\binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}$ over every $k$ no more likely than the observed count. Seven hits in ten gives 0.070, so the verdict `calibrated` means only that the evidence is too thin, while 14 in 20 gives 0.011. Fifty in fifty gives 0.008: ranges that never miss are too wide."

2. `l1-unknown-quantity.json` | `screens[2].explain.text` | imprecise, minor | "Most first guesses say 64 or 65" is an empirical claim about people with no source. | The standard wrong answer in the interview books is 64 (Zhou ch. 2); "most" cannot be established. | "A 3 by 3 square can start in 6 places across and 6 down, so 36 of them. The sizes give 64, 49, 36, 25, 16, 9, 4 and 1, which add to 204. A common first guess is 64 or 65, well below the truth."

3. `l1-unknown-quantity.json` | `screens[10].explain.text` | imprecise, minor | "A price far from your fair value invites the interviewer to trade against you" is false as stated, and the next sentence contradicts it: the price was far from the truth and the trade went the learner's way. | An offer far above fair value deters a buyer. What loses is a price on the wrong side of the true value: an offer below it or a bid above it (Harris ch. 13). | "You took £850 and pay out £792 at settlement, so you make £58. A sell price below the true value would have lost money, so you ask for more than your fair value. Here your price was 58 above the truth, so the trade went your way."

4. `l2-dice-sum.json` | `screens[5].feedback.byKey.product-of-means`; `screens[5].explain.text`; `summary.ideas[2]` | imprecise, minor | "Multiplying fair values works only for independent dice" states a sufficient condition as a necessary one. | E[XY] = E[X]E[Y] exactly when the covariance is zero. X uniform on −1, 0, 1 and Y = X² are dependent with E[XY] = E[X]E[Y] = 0 (L1 script; Casella and Berger Thm 4.5.5, Example 4.5.9). | feedback: "You multiplied the fair values, 3.5 × 3.5. That works for independent dice, and one die is not independent of itself." explain: "Contract B pays 1, 4, 9, 16, 25 or 36, each with chance 1/6: $91/6 \approx 15.17$. Multiplying fair values is safe for independent dice, and it fails here. The gap, $91/6 - 49/4 = 35/12$, is exactly one die’s variance." summary: "Multiply fair values when the dice are independent: one die squared is worth 91/6, not 12.25."

5. `l2-dice-sum.json` | `screens[11].explain.text` | imprecise, minor | "perhaps 9.5 to sell to you and 11.5 to buy from you" is the first two-sided price in the course and its direction can be heard either way. A learner can read it as the maker selling at 9.5. | The maker buys at the lower price and sells at the higher (Harris ch. 13). The sentence also cites a lesson by number. | "The fair value of three fresh dice is 10.5, whatever the last game showed. Your quote sits around it: perhaps you buy at 9.5 and sell at 11.5. A later lesson makes that two-sided price exact."

6. `l3-how-wide.json` | `screens[3].explain.text` | imprecise, minor | "A range should sit on the fair value, where the chance is densest" equates the mean with the mode. The previous lesson taught that they differ. | For the larger of two dice the fair value is 161/36 ≈ 4.47, but the densest range of width 1 is 5 to 6, with 20 of 36 rolls (L1 script). The claim holds for a symmetric one-peaked total. | "Under the width limit, centring on 7 holds 16 rolls. Shifting one step either way holds 15. This total is symmetric, so its fair value is also where the chance is densest, and the range sits there."

7. `l3-how-wide.json` | `screens[11].explain.text` (with `screens[11].prose`; the same desk appears in `review-1.json` `items[6]`) | misleading, minor | The scenario has a desk quoting a £20 share at fair value ±20p, one full daily sd. A learner could carry away that real spreads are about a day's sd. | Quoted spreads in liquid shares are a few hundredths of a per cent of price, roughly a hundred times tighter than 1% (Harris ch. 14). Only the proportional widening with volatility is realistic. The graded keys are right. | "Width follows the sd. Results day triples it, so the quote goes from ±20p to ±60p and holds the same share of moves. Real quotes in a share are far tighter than one day’s sd, but they widen in the same proportion."

8. `l4-fermi.json` | `screens[4].explain.text`; `screens[5].explain.text` (also `screens[5].widget.options[0].text`, `screens[5].hints[2].text`) | imprecise, minor | The total is called "good to a factor of 8" and that range is called honest, with no word that 8 is the worst case. The worst case is named only at `screens[7]`. By the first lesson's own standard a range that almost never misses is too wide. | With three independent errors, each inside a factor of 2 nine times in ten, the product is inside a factor of 2^√3 ≈ 3.3 nine times in ten, and inside a factor of 8 more than 99% of the time (L1 script, 200,000 draws). | `screens[4].explain.text`: "Three factors each off by 2 compound to a factor of $2^3 = 8$ if every guess errs the same way. So the widest range is 10,000 to 640,000 around 80,000. On a log axis each factor adds the same step of doubt." `screens[5].explain.text`: "Three guesses good to a factor of 2 make a total good to a factor of 8 at worst. If the errors are independent some cancel, and the total is usually within a factor of about 3. So \"about 100,000\" is honest and \"95,209\" only looks precise."

9. `review-1.json` | `items[7].explain.text` | wrong, minor | "With the exact figures, 1,609.344 m and 2.25 cm, it is 71,526." The exact figure given is the diameter of the round pound, withdrawn in 2017. The prose of the same item says 2.3 cm. | The 12-sided pound coin is 23.43 mm corner to corner and 23.03 mm edge to edge (Royal Mint specification). 160,934.4 / 2.343 = 68,687.
