2. l4-slope-fields.json | quiz[0].widget.options[2] (and quiz[0].explain.text) | imprecise, minor | For $y' = (y - 2)^2$ the option "It is unstable" is graded wrong, with the reason "Below 2 the slope is positive too, so paths rise towards 2." Under the standard definition an equilibrium is unstable when it is not stable, and it is not stable when some nearby paths leave. Paths that start just above 2 leave, however close they start. So a semi-stable point is unstable, and the option is correct. The course itself uses this sense in l12 screens[7] ("stable but not asymptotically stable"). A learner asked "is $y = 2$ stable?" should answer no. | B&D 9.2 (definitions of stable and unstable); B&D 2.5 and Strogatz 2.2 for semistable and half-stable as a finer label. `odes-L1-concepts-r2.py` `l4_semi_stable_is_lyapunov_unstable` (starts at $2 + 10^{-1}$, $2 + 10^{-2}$, $2 + 10^{-3}$ all pass 2.4) and `EXPECTED_FAIL l4_quiz0_option_unstable_is_wrong`. | options[2].text: "It repels on both sides". options[2].why: "Below 2 the slope is positive, so paths rise towards 2, not away." quiz[0].explain.text: "$(y - 2)^2 > 0$ on both sides, so every nearby path moves up. That is semi-stable, and $f'(2) = 0$ is why the derivative test says nothing. Some nearby paths leave, so in the strict sense it counts as unstable."

## Checked and clean

The probes the brief asked for, and what was found.

- Constant solutions lost on dividing: l2 screens[7] states the condition $h(y) \ne 0$, says every root of $h$ is a constant solution, and puts $P = 0$ back by hand. The manipulate screen notes $D$ is never 0, and the logistic derivation states $P \ne 0$, $P \ne K$ and $0 < P < K$.
- Existence, uniqueness and interval of validity: l1 screens[9] states the hypothesis ($f$ and its derivative in $y$ continuous). l2 screens[5] and quiz[3] state that $1/(1 - t)$ exists only on $t < 1$. Homework-1 uses uniqueness correctly to stop a path crossing an equilibrium, and states $t > 0$ for $ty' + 2y = 4t^2$.
- Integrating factor: $\mu = e^{\int p}$ with $\mu' = p\mu$, both sides multiplied, $\mu$ never 0; the $e^{p}$ and left-side-only slips give the curves and values printed.
- Characteristic equation: all three cases, the repeated root with $te^{rt}$ and why it needs the double root, complex roots with Euler's formula and the real-coefficient argument, the root 0.
- Undetermined coefficients: the three guess families, the $t$ and $t^2$ modification, the zero-root polynomial case, fit after adding. Only the "whole guess" wording is loose (finding 3).
- Resonance: undamped resonance at the natural frequency with $y_p = \tfrac{t}{4}\sin 2t$; the damped peak is put correctly below the natural frequency at $\omega_0\sqrt{1 - 2\zeta^2}$, confirmed by maximising the formula and by simulating the forced equation.
- Damping: three regimes from the discriminant, $\zeta = b/(2\omega_0)$, envelope height $\sqrt{c_1^2 + c_2^2}$ (1.0328 and $\sqrt5/2$), overshoot $e^{-\zeta\pi/\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}}$ against a numerical peak, the settling table recomputed from closed forms, and "critical is the fastest with no overshoot" against every $\zeta$ from 1 to 3.
- Mean reversion: sign of the drift, half-life $\ln 2/\kappa$ acting on the gap, $1/\kappa$ named as the $1/e$ time, the exact AR(1) with $\phi = e^{-\kappa/252}$, and the expected Vasicek path by Monte Carlo (200,000 paths).
- Phase plane: classification by eigenvalues and by trace and determinant agree on 19,000 random matrices; the zero-eigenvalue and equal-eigenvalue cases are named; the direction of turning from the bottom-left entry holds for 5,000 random matrices with complex eigenvalues, and the transpose reverses it.
- Senses of stable: l12 screens[5] defines asymptotic stability, and screens[7] says a centre is stable but not asymptotically stable. Only l4 quiz[0] is at odds with that (finding 2).
- Linearisation: trusted at hyperbolic points only, with Hartman-Grobman stated as "no eigenvalue on the imaginary axis"; the equal-eigenvalue caveat; a linear centre shown to spiral in under cubic terms (and out under the mirror terms); the zero-eigenvalue example. Stability carried over in 150 random hyperbolic systems with quadratic terms.
- Code-behaviour claims: Pyodide's NumPy does return `nan` for a singular solve (run in Pyodide 0.27.7). The float example in l9 does not behave as printed (finding 5).
- Chart curves: every plotted expression read against its prose (loan, logistic, wrong-working curves, envelopes, the three damping branches, step responses, the smoother series, two-factor paths, the trace-determinant points). None contradicts the text.
